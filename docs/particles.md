# 3D particles

pfx's 3D particles are a deterministic emitter simulation in `pfx-core` and a lit, soft, blended billboard pass in the live renderer. A scene declares them with `[[particles]]` ([scenes.md](scenes.md#particles-3d-emitters)); a game can drive the same emitters from code. The flat view's 2D `Burst` ([effects.md](effects.md#bursts)) is the same recipe in layout units; this is it in metres and seconds.

## The simulation: `pfx_core::sim::particles`

An `Emitter` holds a `Recipe`, its attach point `at` (in the parent's local space), a seeded `Rng` and a pool of `Particle`s. The caller steps it with a fixed `dt` and the parent's transform at the start and the end of the step:

```rust
use pfx_core::sim::particles::{Emitter, Recipe, STEP};

let mut spray = Emitter::new(Recipe { rate: 600.0, inherit: 0.3, ..Recipe::default() }, [0.0, -0.3, -0.1]);
spray.step(STEP, wheel_before, wheel_after);
spray.sprites(&mut out);
```

- **Spawning.** Each step adds `rate · dt` (the trapezoid of `rate_keys` over the step when they are set) to a remainder and spawns its whole part, keeping the fraction for the next step, as bevy_hanabi's spawner does; `burst` particles spawn once, on the first step. Spawn `i` of `n` in a step is born at `(i + 0.5) / n` of it: its position is the attach point interpolated between the parent's two transforms at that fraction, and it is integrated over the rest of the step, so a car at 30 m/s leaves an even trail and no gaps between steps.
- **Direction.** A unit vector uniform in a cone of half-angle `cone` (degrees, 0 to 180) around `direction` turned by the parent's rotation, built on Duff et al.'s branchless basis as bevy_hanabi builds its circle basis. Speed is uniform in `speed`; `inherit` adds that share of the attach point's own velocity over the step (the parent's motion, a glTF node's animated motion when the emitter rides a node).
- **Motion.** Each step, as the flat `Burst`: `v = (v + gravity · dt) · exp(−drag · dt)`, `x += v · dt`, `angle += spin · dt`. Spin is uniform in `spin` with a random sign. Particles die at their life, uniform in `lifetime`, and leave by swap-remove.
- **The pool.** `max` caps the live particles; a spawn past it is dropped and counted in `dropped()`.
- **Over life** `t = age / life`: size goes linearly from `size[0]` to `size[1]` (metres across), alpha follows the `alpha` keys and colour the `colours` keys, both sampled as bevy_hanabi's `Gradient::sample` (linear between keys, held before the first and after the last). `Sprite` is what a renderer draws: position, previous position, velocity, size, angle, colour and alpha.
- **Determinism.** No wall clock and no global RNG: the same recipe, seed, steps and parent transforms give the same particles bit for bit. `Emitter::seek(time, step, parent)` steps on a fixed grid from 0 to `time` (resetting when time goes back), so the state at a time depends on that time alone; `STEP` is 1/120 s, which divides 24, 30, 60 and 120 fps frames.
- **Prewarm.** `prewarm` seconds (`Recipe::prewarm`, 0 to `PREWARM_MAX`, 30 s) are simulated before t = 0 on the same 1/120 s grid, as Unity's Prewarm and Godot's `preprocess` simulate before the first frame: the emitter's first step (or a seek to 0) first runs `prewarm / STEP` steps with the parent held at its t = 0 pose and the clock held at 0, so `rate_keys` and the other keys read their t = 0 values and a `burst` fires at −prewarm. The state at time t is then the one an emitter started `prewarm` seconds earlier would have, bit for bit, whatever seeks came before: a frame range or a resumed render stays byte-identical (`prewarm_starts_where_an_emitter_started_that_long_before_with_its_parent_held`). A catch-up that restarts past 120 s skips it, since the window already forms the trails. The live view, play mode and the tracer all seek through it.

Tests (`crates/core/src/sim/tests_particles.rs`): the same seed gives the same particles and seeking matches stepping; inherit carries the parent's velocity and gravity pulls as integrated; the pool cap holds and counts drops; a parent moving a metre in a step leaves an even trail; `rate_keys` drive the rate deterministically.

## Mesh particles

An emitter whose recipe has a `Body` flies its particles as small rigid cards or lumps instead of soft puffs: each particle carries an orientation (a quaternion) and an angular velocity, and the renderers draw it as an instance of a mesh. A scene names that mesh with `mesh` (an object, and with `mesh_node` a glTF node of its mesh); everything else about the emitter (rate, burst, lifetime, cone, inherit, gravity, size and alpha over life) is as above. Any of `mesh`, `mass`, `area`, `flat`, `tumble`, `ground` or `placed` gives the emitter a body.

- **Spawn.** As a sprite, then a uniformly random orientation (Shoemake's three-number quaternion) and an angular velocity on a uniform random axis at a speed uniform in `tumble` (rad/s). These draws come after the sprite's, and only for a body, so an emitter without one draws the same numbers and the same particles as before.
- **Drag by area and mass.** The air pushes on the particle by its velocity against the air (`v − wind`, the wind being the fields below, 0 without one): drag `½ρ A C_D(α) |u| u / m` with ρ = 1.225 kg/m³, `A = area` (one face, m²), `m = mass` (kg), and α the angle between the card and its motion through the air. A card (`flat = 1`) takes the quasi-steady falling-plate model of Andersen, Pesavento and Wang (2005) with Li et al.'s fitted coefficients: `C_D` from 0.1 edge-on to 1.9 broadside, a lift `C_L` across the flow (up to 5.2 sin α while the flow is attached below about 14 degrees, 0.95 sin 2α once it has stalled), and a centre of pressure ahead of the centre that turns the card broadside to its motion, so a falling leaf glides, flutters and tumbles. A lump (`flat = 0`) has a drag coefficient of 1 and no lift or turning; between the two the coefficients blend. The speed against the air is integrated implicitly (`u ← (u + g·dt + lift) / (1 + k C_D |u| dt)`), so a 1 g leaf in a 20 m/s wake stays stable on the 1/120 s grid; a heavy body falls nearly freely (1 kg falls 11.0 m in 1.5 s, a 1 g card of 20 cm² far less). The particle's `drag` key does not apply to a body.
- **Tumble.** The centre of pressure's torque over the card's inertia (`mℓ²/12`, `ℓ = √area`) spins it, and the rotational drag `(1/128) C_D^{π/2} ρ ℓ⁵ ω|ω|` slows it, again implicitly; the spin is held under 30 rad/s.
- **Settling.** With a `ground` height, a particle that reaches it is held on it: its fall bounces back by `bounce` (0 to 1, default 0.1), its sliding and spin keep `1 − friction` (default 0.8) each step on the ground, and it is laid toward flat (its face up or down, whichever is nearer, its heading kept). Once it moves slower than 5 cm/s and the air at it is no faster than `lift` (m/s, default 2), it rests: laid flat, still, and simulated no further, so a street of resting leaves costs only a look-up of the wind at each. With `keep` (default true) a resting particle does not age, so it stays past its life; it ages again once the wind lifts it, which happens when the air at it is faster than `lift`, with a fresh tumble from the seed.
- **Placed.** `placed = [[x, y, z, yaw], ...]` spawns resting particles on the first step at those points (in the parent's space, the yaw in degrees about +Y), as loose copies a wake can stir: fallen leaves or litter along a street.
- **Drawn.** `Emitter::pieces` gives each live particle as a `Piece` (position, orientation and their values one step back, size, colour, alpha, resting); `Piece::matrix` places it, scaled by its size, and `Piece::back(span)` is where it was `span` seconds before for motion vectors. The renderers place the prototype's draws by it: each piece of the prototype keeps its own rotation and scale and is moved so the centre of the prototype's bounds sits at the particle, so `size = [1, 1]` draws it at its own size. The prototype itself is not drawn where it stands. The live renderer adds one instance per piece and draw to the frame's instances, so the mesh's instancing draws a whole emitter in one batch with its own motion vectors; the path tracer places them as instances of the prototype's mesh (one BLAS, many TLAS entries); a traced recipe clip with mesh particles stages a new trace each frame, since its instances change, and draws them without motion blur.

Tests (`crates/core/src/sim/tests_particles.rs`): mesh particles settle on the ground, lie flat and stay there past their life; drag by area and mass slows a light card more than a heavy one, which falls nearly freely; mesh particles in a wake are the same from the same seed and different from another, and the wake moves placed ones.

## Wake fields

`pfx_core::sim::wake::Wake` is a moving object's wake: air dragged along behind it that spreads and fades. It steps on the same 1/120 s grid as the emitters with the object's pose at the start and the end of each step, and every fourth step (30 a second) leaves a sample where its point (`at`, in the object's space) is, with its velocity, unless it stands still. The air at a point is the sum over the samples younger than `length` seconds (default 3) of

`w · (strength · v + (spread · out + rise · up) · |v|)`, `w = e^(−age / decay) · e^(−d² / r²) · (radius / r)² · |v| · Δt / (√π r)`

with `r = radius + grow · age` (metres, defaults 1 and 0.6 m/s), `d` the distance to the sample, `out` the level direction away from its line of travel and `up` +Y; `strength` (0.5), `spread` (0.3), `rise` (0.15) and `decay` (0.8 s) are the recipe's. The last factor is the stretch of path the sample stands for over the Gaussian's line integral, so the field beside a long straight pass is `strength · v` at the moment the object passes whatever the sample spacing, and samples farther than three radii add nothing. Behind the object the air follows it at half its speed and lifts a little; it widens and weakens with time (by `e^(−age / decay) (radius / r)²` on the path) and is gone after `length`. No wall clock: the same poses give the same field (`crates/core/src/sim/tests_wake.rs`: it follows behind and fades with distance, decays with time and is gone after its length, and the same poses give the same field on a fixed grid).

A scene gives an object a wake with `[object.wake]` ([scenes.md](scenes.md#objectwake-a-wake-behind-a-moving-object)), a recipe gives a followed object one with `[follow.wake]` ([recipe-format.md](recipe-format.md)). `Sprays` steps the scene's wakes in lockstep with its emitters, before them in each step, and every emitter moves through all of them: a sprite emitter by its `drag` (`v ← wind + (v + g·dt − wind) · e^(−drag·dt)`, which is the old step when there is no wind), a body by its drag and lift above. So a car's wake is one wake and one emitter of placed leaves, not hundreds of keyed objects.

## In a scene

`pfx_load::scene::Scene::particles` holds each `[[particles]]` table as `Particles { attach, at, recipe, mesh }`. `attach` names the parent object and, with `node`, a glTF node of that object's mesh; `mesh` names the prototype object, with `mesh_node` a node of its mesh, the same way; pfx-load refuses a parent or prototype that is no object of the scene and a node that is not in its mesh, with the file, line and column. `Scene::wakes` holds each object's `[object.wake]` as `Wake { attach, recipe }` (keyed by the object), the node checked the same way. `pfx_load::scene::shapes(scene, draws)` gives each emitter's prototype draws with the matrix that centres them (empty for a sprite emitter), and `shaping` marks the draws the renderers leave out at their own place. A node's world transform is the object's times the node's animated joint matrix when play mode poses it, else the node's own placement in the file.

`pfx_load::scene::sprays::Sprays` (re-exported as `pfx_live::particles::Sprays`) runs a scene's emitters in lockstep on the 1/120 s grid, for the live renderer and the path tracer alike:

- `Staged::frame(aspect, time, seed)` seeks them to `time` with the objects posed by the scene's movers and, when an `[object.animation]` clip plays, the nodes posed by it at each 1/120 s step (`Sprays::seek_rigged`; `Staged::sprays()` exposes them), so the viewer, `pfx gui`'s viewport and anything that renders a `Staged` frame show them at that time; a scene watch reload restarts them when their tables or their parents change.
- Play mode (`pfx-play`'s presenter) steps them from the last presented time to this one, with the parents' transforms interpolated between the last frame's and this frame's poses (glTF node animation included).
- A catch-up longer than 120 s restarts from 120 s before the target, so a far seek stays bounded.
- The path tracer seeks a fresh `Sprays` to the frame's time the way a freshly staged viewer does (`pfx_trace::stage::scene_puffs`), and a traced recipe still or clip runs the recipe's `[[particles]]` through `Sprays::of_emitters` with each emitter's parent posed from the recipe's tracks (`pfx_still::puffs`), so both draw the particles the live view shows at that time ([trace.md](trace.md#particles)).

## The live pass

`Effects::sprays` carries `SprayInstance`s (80 bytes each); the renderer draws them in one instanced pass, `sprays`, after the effects' own particles and before the creatures, at full resolution. A frame with none draws nothing new and allocates nothing: the pass and its pipeline are made on first use, so an existing scene renders byte-identical.

- **Sorting and blending.** Instances are sorted back to front by view depth and drawn with premultiplied blending: `blend = "alpha"` covers what is behind by its alpha, `blend = "add"` writes alpha 0 so it only adds light. Both go through one pipeline.
- **Soft depth fade.** Each fragment fades with `clamp((scene depth − particle depth) / (size / 2), 0, 1)` against the linear depth, so a particle meeting a wall or the road fades out instead of cutting.
- **Velocity stretch.** With `stretch > 0` the quad follows the particle's motion on screen: its velocity relative to the camera (its position now against where the last frame's camera saw it, carried back into this frame's view), with the part along the eye's ray to it taken away. The quad turns to that, lengthens by `stretch · speed` metres (seconds of travel) and stays unspun; otherwise it spins by its angle. A chase camera moving with the spray therefore streaks it by the spray's motion against the car, outward from the centre as it falls back toward the lens, not by its world speed. `stretch` is seconds: 0.01 to 0.05 gives the streak of one to three frames at 60 fps; at a few seconds a spray thrown back at 20 m/s draws as streaks tens of metres long.
- **Lit.** A lit particle is a puff of scattering medium. Where it is thin it scatters by the Henyey-Greenstein phase of the angle between the eye's ray and the light (`g` is the outdoor haze's `phase`, 0.5 without one, the haze's default), so mist with the sun or a lamp behind it glows toward the eye as the haze around it does; where it is dense (its alpha at that fragment) it shades a sphere normal across its disc with half-wrapped diffuse, `max(n·l + 0.5, 0) / 2.25π`, which integrates to 1 over the sphere as the tracer's lobe does (it was divided by 1.5π before, 1.5 times the light it receives). The pass is single scattering: light a puff scatters toward another is not drawn, which the tracer draws (see [trace.md](trace.md), Particles). The sun comes through its shadow cascades (a 2 × 2 tap of the cascade the particle's view depth picks), leaking the haze's own `1 − shafts` into shadow when the scene has an outdoor haze, so spray in a street's shade is lit like the haze it sits in. Ambient is the probe grid's irradiance where the scene has probes and the sky's spherical harmonics where it has none, over π, averaged over the two faces where thin. Up to 16 local lights add with the opaque pass's falloff (`window² / max(d, radius)²`) and spot cone, unshadowed. All of it times `colour`. Unlit particles emit their colour.
- **Motion vectors.** A second target blends an unstretched particle's own screen motion into the velocity buffer, weighted by its alpha (`SrcAlpha, OneMinusSrcAlpha` on `Rg16Float`); the motion is the corner's current position under the unjittered view projection against its position one frame back (`position − velocity · span`, `span` the time since the last frame `Sprays` was sought to, up to 0.1 s) under the last frame's, as the opaque prepass writes it. The motion blur and TAA passes read that buffer, so sprays blur along their own motion. A stretched particle writes no motion: its streak is its blur, and its motion blended into the pixels under it would smear the road behind it into spikes.
- **Bindings.** Group 0 holds the frame uniform, the linear depth, the instances, the shadow atlas and its uniform; group 3 the probe grid's own layout (`ProbeLighting::layout_entries`), with groups 1 and 2 empty. `crates/live/tests/binding_floor.rs` counts it as `live spray layout`. Only floor features: storage in the vertex stage, blending on `Rgba16Float` and `Rg16Float`.

The GPU tests in `crates/live/tests/sprays.rs` (under pgpu): `particles_draw_lit_and_blended` checks that black alpha particles cover a glowing wall, that additive particles only add, and that lit particles brighten with the sun; `backlit_mist_is_lighter_than_the_bright_wall_behind_it` puts light grey mist (alpha 0.25) between the eye and a low sun in front of a bright wall and wants it lighter than the wall (0.40 bare, 1.97 misted; 0.03, darker, before the phase); `stretch_follows_the_screen_motion` wants a particle coming at the eye right of centre streaked across the screen (16 × 4 px; a 4 × 4 dot before) and a rising one streaked up (4 × 46 px).

## Cost

Measured once with `spray_cost_at_2160x3840` (`cargo test --release -p pfx-live --test sprays -- --ignored spray_cost --nocapture` under `pgpu run --class bench`), a road spray at the shot's 2160 × 3840: particles 0.08 to 0.2 m across, lit, alpha 0.5, velocity-stretched, spread over a few metres about 5 m from the camera.

| Live particles | GPU `sprays` pass, median (p10 to p90) | CPU step and pack, median |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 2,000 | 0.58 ms (0.16 to 1.37) | 0.05 ms |
| 20,000 | 5.37 ms (1.54 to 13.05) | 0.42 ms |

The cost is overdraw: the 100 timed frames run from 1.3 to 3 s of scene time, as the spray thrown toward the camera spreads and falls, and the frames where it fills more of the screen are the slow tail. Fill, not count, sets the cost; the phase lighting and the two-sided ambient cost about a quarter more than the diffuse-only shading did (0.45 and 4.20 ms); the CPU side (two 1/120 s steps and the sprite list packed into 80-byte instances; the renderer then sorts them back to front) stays near 22 ns a particle. Measured on this machine's AMD RDNA 4 under Linux; other vendors are built but untested here.
