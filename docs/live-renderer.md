# Live renderer design

Status: design for the live renderer, 2026-10-01. The implementation target is wgpu 27 on an RX 9060 XT with 16 GiB. All timings, scene limits and package sizes below are **engineering estimates**, not measurements on the new renderer. The existing apps provide behavior and look references, not a 4K performance baseline.

## Contract and frame budget

Render the scene at 3840×2160, present at a steady 60 Hz, and retain fine silhouettes, print, and specular detail during motion. No live path tracing, including a hidden low-resolution full-screen trace. The offline path tracer remains the reference for stills, clips and bakes. Its cameras (perspective, orthographic and equirectangular) and the staging of one scene for both renderers are in [`trace.md`](trace.md). A bounded raster pass for transparent materials is part of the live renderer; a small SDF pass is an optional migration bridge, not the final default.

One frame is 16.67 ms. The initial target is at most 14.5 ms of serialized GPU work, leaving 2.17 ms for variance, queue and present. CPU scene update, command preparation and submission should complete within 3 ms and overlap GPU work. The pass allocation is a **budget estimate** to be replaced with wgpu timestamp measurements on the target GPU:

The measured column is the engine's own bench (`crates/live/examples/bench/`, "The engine bench" below) at 3840×2160 and 16:00 with its probe and cube bakes on, on the RX 9060 XT under RADV: the mean of each pass over 600 frames at rest with the camera director moving, capped at 60 fps. The renderer reports the 16 costliest passes of a frame, so a cheap pass may be missing. The bench's whole frame at 4K is 16.6 ms at the median at rest (60.3 fps) and 19.0 ms moving (52.6 fps), with 10.7 to 11.3 ms in the 16 timed passes. Capped at 60 fps a pass reads up to 1.7× longer than at the GPU's fast clock, because RADV drops its clock between frames; the paired modes below keep only the pairs at the fast clock.

The estimate starts with 8,294,400 pixels/frame: one RGBA16F 4K surface is 63.3 MiB and a full read plus write is about 126.6 MiB before cache effects. Full-screen passes, overdraw, shadow fill and transparency make the real cost scene-dependent. It assumes one opaque shading pass, reduced-resolution bloom/volume, indexed geometry, resident textures and no per-frame bake. It does **not** extrapolate a 1440p or 1586×992 result to 4K. Each pass gets GPU timestamp markers; measure median, p95 and p99 GPU and display frame times after warm-up, while moving the camera, turning the page, changing daylight and animating liquid. A shippable preset requires p99 display intervals at or below 16.67 ms over a five-minute representative run with no 33 ms frames caused by the renderer, on the stated GPU/driver and shipping content. Record memory, visible triangles, texture misses, upload bytes and image quality alongside timing. If the scene exceeds a budget, reduce invisible geometry, effect density or expensive reflection coverage before touching native pixel density or text sharpness.

### The engine bench

`crates/live/examples/bench/` is the engine's own measuring scene, and the numbers in this section come from it. It reads nothing outside the engine: the room is built by seeded code, its materials come from the bench's own `materials.toml`, its fonts are the engine's EB Garamond with the Noto Sans SC subset as the CJK fallback, and its pictures, video, cards and caustics are painted procedurally at start.

- **The room.** A 6 m × 5.4 m × 2.8 m box of plaster walls, a plank floor and a ceiling, with a window in the left wall closed by ten tilted slats, a 3.6 m table, three shelves of objects on the back wall and a dark open cavity on the floor. At 16:00 the sun comes in through the slats at 37° and lands on the left end of the table, where a sloped drafting board, three small contact objects, a tinted glass slab and a glass lens stand in the beam. 141 parts: 112 on three shared meshes (62 on a cube and 26 on a can, both with a UV1 atlas, and 24 on a sphere) and 28 sheets and cards on a page mesh with a second UV set, with a perforated grille (cutout), an awning outside the window (shadow only), a lamp and two small boxes that cast no shadow, a rolled sheet on a `Roller` deformer, and a seeded tree outside the window with the procedural canopy and petals. Every material family is on screen but one, the sheet family whose name the agnostic check denies in new files until it is renamed, and so is every detail kind: plank wood, grime, scratch, fibre, crinkle, coat wobble (as a kind and as a bump), wall mottle, leaf, bark, value and fBm noise, with ageing, patina and thin film.
- **Glass and the rest.** A clear sheet, the tinted slab casting a tinted shadow, a glass tank with rippling water and a plume above it, the lens with a caustic map traced on the CPU through a refracting sphere (`maps::Caustic`), a milky slab, a soap-film sphere and a filmed slab; room haze with the room's bounds, 1,200 motes in the sunbeam, three birds outside the window, two impostor props, surface, lit, deformed and glancing MSDF text, an overlay title, rich spans, a CJK line through the fallback, and three numbers that change every frame on the shared atlas's fast path; a procedural photo, a 90-frame procedural video uploaded per frame and a content atlas of 216 cards, an ink page and a decal.
- **Light.** The light is the bench's own `pfx bake` recipe, `bench/bake.toml`: app `bench`, the solar sun at day 172, 45° N, with the daylight sky fill, anchors at 12:00, 16:00 and 19:00 and noon as the reference hour. The bench reads it through the bake library's `SceneSource`, exactly as `pfx bake` does, and lights, bakes and traces the room from it, so `pfx bake` of the recipe and the bench's own bake are the same scene (a test checks the triangles and every material). The recipe sits beside `room.glb` (the static parts, which the bench writes itself) and `materials.toml` (a bake-safe material `Library`; content slots are bound by the bench at run time). It holds a 21×11×20 probe grid every 0.3 m, two local probe volumes (the table at 0.15 m and the cavity at 0.07 m) and two reflection cubes (table and shelves); the bench caches them under `tmp/bench/` by scene hash, and the whole bake took 8.2 minutes with the GPU to itself. `BENCH_RECIPE=write` rewrites the three files and a test fails when they drift from the code; a GPU test bakes the cavity volume and the table cube twice and finds every file byte-identical.
- **References.** `bench/reference.csv` is the engine's own trace of the staged room (bare materials, glass, no tree, text or volume) at 1280×800 and 256 samples per pixel, sliced by the Pacer, kept as the mean linear colour of every part with at least 200 interior pixels and of a 16×10 grid of screen cells. `BENCH_REFERENCE=trace` traces it again; `BENCH_REFERENCE=compare` renders the same staging live and prints how far each region's luma sits from the trace.

Run it after a release build, under the queue: `cargo build --release -p pfx-live --example bench`, then `pgpu run --class clip --as pfx -- target/release/examples/bench`. Every mode caps itself at 60 fps with turns between frames; `PFX_UNCAPPED=1` lifts the cap. `BENCH_SIZE=deck` draws at 1280×800 instead of 3840×2160.

## Frame graph and geometry

The app submits a scene snapshot: camera, time, seed, daylight, meshes or analytic primitives, instances, deformers, material IDs, print/image layers and prior transforms. The engine owns GPU resources and produces depth, velocity, HDR color and display output. Preserve stable IDs across frames for TAA and picking. Rasterize opaque surfaces first, then cutouts, then transparent surfaces and effects. Use reversed-Z depth if the selected wgpu depth format and app cameras permit it; validate precision at the actual near/far planes. Frustum/occlusion culling uses previous-frame depth conservatively so newly visible objects cannot disappear.

Ship indexed triangle meshes with normal/tangent and stable UVs. Generate several LODs offline from the same smooth source, with bounded normal-cone and UV error. Choose per object by projected geometric error: at 4K, target less than 0.5 pixel silhouette displacement and less than 1 pixel interior curvature error; use a 20% hysteresis band and crossfade or geomorph where switching would pop. This is a visual error criterion, not a fixed polygon count. Subdivide thin round branches and tubes based on projected circumference (roughly 2–4 pixels per segment at the closest supported view), so 8–10-sided branches are not the final 4K mesh. Use smooth normals only where the shape is genuinely smooth; sharp edges retain split normals. Profile culling, vertex work and shadow LOD separately, and cap visible scene triangles initially at about 2 million per view as a tuning hypothesis.

Analytic slabs, beads, lenses, icons, tubes, ropes and filleted metal become parameterized meshes generated from the analytic dimensions; tessellate fillets by the same projected-error rule. Keep analytic parameters in the shared scene so the offline path tracer can render the exact SDF shape. A liquid heightfield becomes a dynamic grid mesh with normals from the simulation texture, refined near the camera; its simulation and ripples remain live. Glass and liquid use raster entry/exit depth, thickness, Beer–Lambert absorption, Fresnel reflection and screen-space refraction. For self-overlap that two depth layers cannot represent, allow a **scissored** SDF fragment pass limited to glass/liquid bounds, with a hard 1.0 ms ceiling and at most two refractive crossings per pixel. If that limit fails, replace the case with explicit front/back meshes or a precomputed proxy; do not trace the whole screen. Chrome remains opaque raster PBR. A floor caustic or light bake can become an offline caustic texture for fixed optics plus a bounded live caustic or projected decal for moving lenses and liquid.

Deform content and its surface from one rest-space parameterization. A paper scroll uses a strip mesh with rest `(u,v)` UVs; the roller maps both its positions and tangents into world space every frame, while ink/text/image samples retain rest UVs. Subdivide by projected curvature error and at glyph edges; carry previous deformed positions into velocity output. Do not re-rasterize all print when the scroll moves. Bending, page clipping, normal maps, shadows, selection and picking must agree on that one mapping. At folds or overlaps, depth and two-sided lighting decide which side is visible; the back has its own paper/print response.

Uploaded meshes can be freed and edited. `Renderer::release_mesh(handle)` frees the mesh's vertex, position and index buffers and its sway range, and returns the slot to a free list, so upload and release in a loop reuses slots and keeps `mesh_stats()` (live meshes, slots, buffers) bounded. `MeshStats.buffers` counts mesh buffers by their own life: each vertex, position and index buffer is wrapped in a counter raised when it is created and lowered when it is dropped, so a replace that grows a mesh and drops its old buffers, or a release, shows in the count as it happens rather than as three per live slot. `Renderer::replace_mesh(handle, data)` swaps a mesh's data under the same handle, writing into the existing buffers when the new data fits and allocating new ones only when it is larger; it also resets TAA history, and a swaying mesh can be released but not replaced. A `MeshHandle` keeps its `u32` shape, now a 20-bit slot and a 12-bit generation (`MeshHandle(n)` is slot `n`, generation 0): a released handle is refused by `release_mesh` and `replace_mesh`, `shadow_mesh` and `swaying` see it as absent, and an instance that still points at it makes `render` return an error naming a released mesh instead of drawing another mesh. A slot whose generation reaches 4095 retires, so a handle never aliases. The LOD set owns the meshes it uploads.

Glass meshes follow the same model. `Renderer::upload_glass(&Mesh)` returns a `glass::MeshHandle`, now a `u32` with a 20-bit slot and a 12-bit generation like `frame::MeshHandle` (`MeshHandle(n)` is slot `n`, generation 0). `Renderer::release_glass(handle)` frees the mesh's vertex and index buffers and puts the slot on a free list, so upload and release in a loop reuse slots. `Renderer::replace_glass(handle, &Mesh)` swaps the data under the same handle: it writes into the existing buffers when the new vertices and indices fit and allocates new ones, dropping the old, only when the mesh grew; it also resets TAA history. A stale handle (released, or from a retired slot) is refused by `release_glass` and `replace_glass`, `glass_mesh_buffers` returns `None` for it, and a `glass::Surface` that still points at it makes `render` return an error naming a missing or released mesh instead of drawing another mesh. A slot whose generation reaches 4095 retires. `MeshStats` gains `glass_live`, `glass_slots` and `glass_buffers`, counted by the buffers' own life exactly as `buffers` is, so a flat-memory test sees a glass leak. The renderer keeps no CPU copy of a glass mesh: a resize rebuilds the glass pass's targets but hands its mesh storage, handles and all, to the new pass, because the meshes do not depend on the frame size, so a mesh is released once and nothing else holds it.

## Shared materials and ageing

One versioned, linear-light material description drives the live shader and offline path tracer. Its core fields are base color, opacity, metallic, perceptual roughness, specular F0 or IOR, normal/detail, clearcoat/coat roughness, transmission, thickness, absorption color/distance, subsurface or thin-sheet translucency, emission, and UV texture references. The evaluators share parameter meanings and texture transforms, even where the live approximation differs from traced transport. Direct diffuse/specular use an energy-conserving GGX microfacet model, with multiple-scattering compensation checked against the offline reference. The path tracer owns true multi-bounce transport; live indirect diffuse comes from bakes and specular from reflection sources.

| Surface | Live interpretation |
|---|---|
| Paper | Thin-sheet transmission and backlighting controlled by thickness/fiber map; printed ink is a separate absorbing layer in rest UVs. |
| Glass | Dielectric Fresnel, thickness-dependent absorption, bounded screen refraction and fallback environment where off-screen. |
| Chrome/metal | Colored conductor F0, low roughness, reflection hierarchy; no diffuse base on fully metallic regions. |
| Liquid | Dynamic normal/thickness, dielectric reflection/refraction, dye absorption and optional scattering; caustic response is a separate light contribution. |
| Wood | Base/normal/roughness textures, grain direction and optional anisotropy; diffuse with restrained coat. |
| Lacquer | Dielectric clearcoat above substrate, distinct coat roughness and normal. |
| Emissive | Physical radiance in linear HDR; contributes to bake when static, bloom only as a display effect. |

Procedural noise layers fade the octaves a pixel can't show. Each octave's rate (cycles per metre) times the pixel's footprint on the surface gives the octave's footprint in noise cells per pixel; the derivatives are taken once per fragment before any discard (`map_footprint` in `maps.rs`). The fade is the materials crate's `octave_weight`, the one `fbm3` and `value_noise3_gradient` already use: an octave is whole while its wavelength is at least two pixels (under 0.5 cells per pixel), fades smoothly to its mean as the wavelength falls to one pixel, and is not evaluated at all below that, so a far part costs less. Close detail is unchanged; the GPU test `tests/noise_footprint.rs`, with the hash noise forced for the kinds that now read tiles, finds near pixels within 0.04/255 of the unfaded shader on the six parameterised noise kinds, and far pixels collapse onto their mean. Value noise fades to 0.5; nonlinear terms fade to the mean of the whole expression over the CPU noise: the plank wood's ring term to 0.25 (its rings fade from 8/7 of the ring frequency, `RING_FADE`), the scratch line to 0.0897 and its specks to 0.0015, the grime's wear to 0.3814 (the faded grime's far mean is 0.23026 against 0.23029 unfaded). The wood's plank tones and seams are not octaves and are not faded. The tracer averages many samples per pixel, so it sees these means. The footprint is one float per fragment, the longer of the object position's two screen derivatives, times each octave's largest rate; weighing every octave against both derivative vectors cost more than it saved. On a desk scene at 4K the fade saves 0.06 to 0.14 ms of the opaque pass with a mean change of 0.08/255, because a 4K pixel there covers 0.2 to 0.3 mm and few octaves are finer. `OpaqueFeatures::fine_noise` (off unless forced on with `Frame::set_opaque_on`) evaluates every octave as before, for comparison.

The coat wobble samples a tiling noise texture instead of hashing (`lacquer.rs`, `map_lacquer` in `maps.rs`). A `CoatWobble` layer carries three octave frequencies, fine then two broad, and the two broad octaves' weights relative to the fine one, whose weight is the layer's amplitude. The tile is 512² RGBA8: red and green hold the two broad octaves' gradient summed with their weights over a 1 m period (the frequencies rounded to whole lattice cells so the tile wraps), blue and alpha a fine gradient over 64 cells that the shader reads at the layer's fine frequency divided by 64. Each value is the central difference `value_noise3_gradient` takes (±0.35 of a cell) of a periodic 2D value noise on `hash3` (`Hash::Xor`), in cell units, times √(26/35) = 0.862: a plane through the 3D noise blends two lattice layers by the smoothstep of its height, and 26/35 is that blend's mean variance, so the tile keeps the bump's strength on average over planes. The ranges are ±2.5 and ±1.5. `lacquer::bytes(&coat)` bakes it on the CPU; `MapTextures` starts with the neutral `CoatWobble::default()` (no broad octaves) and `MapTextures::fit_coat` rebuilds the layer from the first active coat wobble among a frame's materials before the frame draws, so a product passing its own coat gets its own tile. A coat whose parameters differ from the tile's takes the hash noise. The fine channel is shared with the other kinds' bumps and never carries a weight. It rides a spare layer of the `metal` array (group 3, binding 3), so no binding is added and the floor stays at 18 sampled textures: `MapTextures` puts it after the app's metal maps when there are none or they are 512², names the layer in `MapTextures::noise`, and `MapTextures::material` writes it into the map material's `packed.z`. With other metal map sizes `noise` is `None`, `packed.z` is −1 and the coat keeps its hash noise. `NormalSource::Bump` no longer adds a coat of its own; a bump is a `CoatWobble` layer.

The shader projects the tile three ways in object space. `map_footprint` keeps the object position's screen derivatives for lacquer pipelines; their cross product is the face's object-space normal, so the projection follows the object through any motion. Each axis whose share of that normal is over 0.5 takes part, weighted by the excess and normalized, so a face within 30° of an axis reads one plane, and a face between two axes blends two with the sum divided by the root of the weights' squares, which keeps the bump's variance. A plane is two `textureSampleGrad` reads through the 8× anisotropic map sampler, one for the broad octaves at the position in metres and one for the fine octave at 14.84375 times it, with the derivatives as gradients, so the reads stay correct where neighbouring pixels take different planes. Two features specialize it: `lacquer` is on only for parts with a lacquer layer or the bump normal, so no other part compiles any of it, and `procedural_lacquer` (off unless forced on) puts the hash noise back for comparison. Measured on the CPU, the filtered tile is within 1.2% (broad) and 2.0% (fine) rms of the exact periodic gradients, and over 25,600 points on 64 random planes its bump has 1.011 times the hash noise's rms. The GPU test `tests/lacquer_tile.rs` renders a lacquer plane at 24 places against the hash noise: the shading's bump has 1.006 times its rms where every octave is resolved and 0.996 where the 950/m octave is under a pixel, and the bump's mean darkening agrees within 10%; frames of parts without lacquer are byte-identical with the tile, without it and with the hash noise forced. The tile is a different realization of the same statistics, so it matches the hash noise in strength and grain, not pixel for pixel. Per plane the hash noise's strength varies with the plane's height in the lattice, between 0.71 and 1 of a 2D noise's; the tile has the mean 0.86, so a single flat part can read a little stronger or weaker than before. The path tracer keeps the hash noise.

Four more noise kinds read their octaves from tiles (`noise_tiles.rs`, `tile_noise` and `tile_wobble` in `maps.rs`): the scratch's line and smudge, the wall's fine albedo octaves (420 and 1500/m) and its bump, the paper fibre's three value octaves and its bump, and the crinkle's bump.  Two more 512² RGBA8 layers follow the lacquer's in the `metal` array (at `packed.z + 1` and `+ 2`), each holding two octaves, and each octave is two channels: lattice layers A and B of a 64-cell periodic 2D value noise (8 texels per cell). A 3D value noise is the smoothstep blend of two 2D lattice layers by the fractional depth, so an octave at depth `d` is `mix(A, B, fade(fract(d)))`, with A and B swapped on odd cells, which makes the tile a 3D noise periodic over two cells in depth with the hash noise's statistics plane by plane, not only on average. The lattice values are `hash3` (`Hash::Xor`) ranked and set to the exact uniform quantiles `(rank + 0.5) / 4096`, so each octave's mean is 0.5 and its variance the uniform's; the raw hashes of a 64² lattice sat 0.003 off and shifted the smudge's tail by 6%. A value octave projects on the face's planes as the lacquer does, sums the planes' deviations from 0.5 weighted and normalized to keep the variance, and its third axis is the depth; anisotropic rates (the scratch line's stretch, `SCRATCH_STRETCH` of its frequency across, and the fibre's across and along frequencies) scale the coordinates and the gradients alike. A bump octave reads the lacquer tile's fine gradient (blue and alpha, 64 cells) at its own rate with its own offset. Each read is one `textureSampleGrad`, and the arrays' box-filtered mips take the place of the per-octave footprint fade; an octave under the pixel is still skipped where the hash noise skipped it, and the scratch's shaped line and the grime and wood keep their fades to the expression's mean. The specks stay a single hash. On the CPU the filtered tiles are within 1.3% rms of the exact octaves, each octave's mean and deviation are within 0.5% and 1% of the hash noise's over 80,000 points on 400 planes, and the scratch line covers 0.088 and 0.090 of a side and a top face against 0.089 and 0.090 hashed; a test pins both layers' SHA-256. The GPU test `tests/noise_tiles.rs` renders each kind on a plane at 96 places against the hash noise: the near detail's spread is 0.89 to 1.0 of the hash noise's and its mean moves by at most 0.16/255, far pixels agree within 0.02/255, and without the tiles each kind is the hash noise to within 0.06/255 in a handful of values. Grime (5.5 mm blotches on 2 cm hubs), the wood's rings (warped at 0.8 to 6 cycles per metre over the desk) and the wall's broad blotches (2.6 and 7.5/m) keep the hash noise: on tiles each would be a different realization at the scale of its part, the grime and wood cost 0.02 and 0.04 ms, and the wall's two broad octaves moved its mean by 0.86/255. `procedural_detail` (off unless forced on) puts the hash noise back for every tiled kind; without the tile layers (metal maps of another size) the kinds keep it.

A thin film (`thin_film`, `thin_film_ior`, `thin_film_amount`) shows on opaque, metal and glass surfaces alike. Its reflectance is Airy's closed form over 24 wavelengths, both polarisations, weighted into linear sRGB, within 1/255 of the path tracer's 81-wavelength film from 0 to 1 µm on dielectrics and 0 to 400 nm on metals, at 0 to 70°; a 1 µm film steps no more between neighbouring thicknesses than the tracer's does, where the old three-wavelength film banded. Under a dielectric material the film sits on a substrate of the material's `ior` (a lacquer with an oil slick), under a metal on a conductor whose complex index comes from its colour as Cycles derives it (anodised titanium). `OpaqueFeatures::film` compiles it only into parts that carry it, so other parts' frames are the same bytes, and the opaque pass evaluates it twice per pixel, at the view angle and at normal incidence, for the sun, the local lights and the environment. At 4K a frame covered in film costs 1.3 ms more over a dielectric and 3.8 ms more over a metal. `docs/trace-film.md` has the model, the conductor's index, the choice of 24 wavelengths and the checks.

Age is caller-supplied `0..1`, never inferred from the wall clock. Material recipes map it to yellowing and loss of brightness, ink fading, coat roughening, scratch coverage and worn-edge masks from curvature/AO plus seeded texture fields. Use asset-stable seeds and object-space coordinates, so ageing does not swim during animation or vary with frame rate. The bake manifest records the age state used for static indirect light; if ageing materially changes albedo/emission, use discrete bake variants or rebake offline. Continuous age changes shade live, with a documented approximation between bake anchors.

## Daylight, baked light and reflections

The shared daylight input supplies sun direction, angular radius, color/intensity and sky state. Render three practical-split cascaded shadow maps at an initial 2048² depth each, with stable texel snapping; cover the camera's useful near/mid/far ranges, not an arbitrary whole world. Use PCF filtering (initially 3×3 taps) plus receiver-plane bias tuned by slope; contact hardening may use 5×5 only in near cascade. Add a low-cost contact shadow term for small gaps. Alpha-tested leaf cards cast shadows, with wind moving their vertices and velocity; deterministic wind time/seed drives animated dappled light on surfaces. Sun and moving leaf shadow are live, so turning the hour never requires a full path trace. Moving objects cast live shadows and receive both direct and baked indirect light.

`pfx bake` stores **indirect** diffuse irradiance and stationary emissive contribution, with direct sun and moving occluders excluded. Static UV-unwrapped surfaces use directional lightmaps where unique UVs exist. Movable objects and deformable paper use a leak-resistant probe volume with visibility/normal weighting and fallback sky at boundaries. A 45×12×10 grid has 5,400 probes and six directional lobes, updated from two rays per probe over time; it is a good spatial starting point, but live ray accumulation and noisy convergence are replaced by offline, higher-sample bakes. Six RGB half-float lobes padded to 8 bytes each cost about 0.25 MiB per 5,400-probe grid, before visibility data. Probe spacing follows geometry and leakage tests; a large scene needs sparse bricks, not a uniform grid across empty space.

For a changing hour, keep direct sun and shadow maps live and interpolate between precomputed indirect-light anchors. Start with four anchors across the supported daylight range, add anchors where a test image deviates visibly from the offline reference, and include night/indoor lighting states if the product uses them. An hour change selects/interpolates resident anchors and updates sun/sky uniforms; **nothing is path traced during interaction**. Rebuild offline when static geometry, important static material/age, sky/HDR environment or stationary emitters change. Dynamic props and leaves are never baked into static occlusion. Lightmaps must store irradiance, not already-multiplied albedo, so material edits remain meaningful within the stated bake error.

Reflection priority is: valid screen-space reflection for on-screen detail, local prefiltered reflection probes for off-screen and rough surfaces, then the sky. Capture local probes offline from the same scene/hour anchors; moving objects receive live direct highlights and approximate local reflections. Chrome/glass quality gates include edge-of-screen, disocclusion and mirrorlike surfaces. Use a small planar reflection only for a product surface that demonstrably needs it and fits the reflection budget; do not use full-scene live rays. Blend probe seams and clamp invalid SSR history to avoid black gaps.

### Probe lookup

`probes::PROBE_WGSL` reads the baked grid for a surface at `position` with normal `n`. It offsets the point 6 mm along `n` and takes the eight probes of its cell, trilinearly weighted less the corner floor (`set_corner_floor`, 0.05). Each probe is weighed by how much it faces the surface (`((cos + 1) / 2)³`). A version 2 probe also needs its validity flag set (the bake disables a probe whose rays mostly hit back faces, and may first move it up to half a spacing out of the geometry), and passes a Chebyshev test of its stored depth moments in the direction of the point, with a bias and a variance floor of a tenth of the spacing. Each probe's six lobes are evaluated as an ambient cube along `n`. With no probe left, the lookup falls back to the sky's SH.

**Local volumes.** `ProbeLighting::from_artifacts(main, locals, hour, fallback)` adds up to `PROBE_LOCALS` (4) finer volumes, each a schema 2 artifact baked from the same recipe (another `[[volumes]]` entry) with the main volume's anchors, at least three probes on every axis and no emitter layer. Their probes ride in the same anchor buffers after the main grid's, and the probe uniform carries each one's origin, spacing, dimensions and word offset, so the bindings are unchanged. A point inside a local volume is lit by it alone; across its outer spacing it fades into the main grid, and a local volume that finds no probe for a point leaves it to the main grid. The first local volume that holds the point wins. Without local volumes the lookup is the main grid's, as before.

**Reflection occlusion.** `ProbeLighting::set_reflection_occlusion(threshold)` (0 by default, off) scales the opaque pass's specular reflection by the probes' irradiance at the surface over the irradiance its reflection source implies along `n`: the sky's SH for the sky's share, and π times each local reflection cube's last mip for theirs. A surface whose probes see at least `threshold` of that keeps all its reflection; below it, the reflection fades in proportion, and an SSR hit keeps its own confidence's share. A cube with distances (wave 68) is read along `n` itself for that reference: its last mip is close to an ambient cube, and a second march per pixel cost the opaque pass 0.6 ms at 4K for no visible change. Off, the opaque pass skips it entirely, so frames are as before. `Renderer::set_reflection_occlusion(threshold)` (and `Frame`'s) changes it on a live renderer, without reloading the probes.

**Per instance.** `InstanceSurface::reflection_occlusion` (1 by default) is how much of that occlusion an instance takes: 0 keeps its whole reflection, 0.5 half way. It rides in the instance's flags word as an 8-bit release, 0 at the default, so the instance buffer and every frame are unchanged unless an instance asks; a value outside 0 to 1 or not a number is refused by `set_surfaces`. It is for a glossy part that faces the open room from a spot the probes cannot resolve: the occlusion trusts the probes' irradiance, and where the probes sit inside or behind thin geometry they read dark for a surface that is in fact open.

**Why, on a desk scene (wave 32).** A product's desk read 1.35 to 2.65 times its 512-sample trace in two recesses, the reel bays' slider channel and the ruler under the timeline lip. Measured at the product's camera with its room sky and committed bake, 162 surface points against probes traced at the points themselves: the felt channel's probe irradiance was 0.90 to 0.93 of the truth, and the lip's 1.17 to 1.36, all of it a 45° bevel tucked under the lip (2.7× at every hour), where all eight probes of the cell are on the far side of the lip and the nearest, 8 mm away, takes 94% of the weight because the stored moments (four rays per face) and the 5 mm bias cannot see a gap that small. Tightening the Chebyshev bias and floor to 0.02 of the spacing moved the lip by 3% at most, and even exact ray visibility to each probe left the bevel 0.28 to 1.5 of the truth. In the image, though, diffuse light was not the leak: drawn with every material's specular off, both regions read 0.71 to 0.87 of the trace, and the sun reaches neither. The excess was the reflection cube and the sky lighting a recess as if it were open. With reflection occlusion at 0.5, the regions read 0.95, 0.88 and 0.91 (felt) and 0.89, 0.98 and 0.92 (lip) of the trace at 12, 16 and 19 h, the wall and page controls move by 0.01 at most, and over the whole frame the mean absolute error against the trace falls by 25 to 27% and the share of pixels over 1.5× the trace from 23–28% to 14–17%. A threshold of 1 overshoots (0.81 to 0.93). That product turns it on with `set_reflection_occlusion(0.5)`; the lip's bevel needs a local volume for its diffuse light, and `a_local_volume_keeps_the_light_out_of_a_slotted_box` pins that: a 4 cm box with a 1 cm slot, between the probes of a 5 cm grid, reads far above its traced interior from the main grid alone and within 1.5× with a 1 cm local volume.

**What it costs and where it is wrong (wave 49).** Paired at 3840×2160 on that desk with its bake (300 frame pairs, each pair one frame with the term and one without), the occlusion at 0.5 costs the opaque pass 0.32 ms at rest and 0.31 ms moving (p50 of the per-pair difference; 10.73 against 10.42 ms), the 1 cm lip volume 0.22 and 0.20 ms, and the per-instance scale nothing measurable (−0.02 ms). The term trusts the probes' irradiance along `n`, and that fails for a glossy part whose mirror direction is open while its hemisphere is not, or whose cell's probes sit behind or inside thin geometry: on that desk a card strip's curl reads from probes in the 1 cm gap behind the strip (their +z depth is 5 mm), and a film strip's left end from probes buried in the chrome slider block (all six lobes zero, still enabled). Neither a reference without the parallax correction nor a 1 cm volume over the film fixed them (the film's left end stayed at 0.02 to 0.17 of the trace), because the hemisphere really is darker there; only the reflection direction is open. Those instances set `reflection_occlusion` to 0.

### The environment BRDF (wave 69)

`reflection_environment` scales each reflection source by the split-sum environment BRDF, `f0 · A + B`. It used Karis's analytic mobile approximation, and against the tracer's GGX (α = roughness², separable Smith, roughness floored at 0.05) that approximation loses light on every metal: `A + B` at F0 = 1 reads 0.945 against 1.000 at roughness 0.1, 0.81 against 0.97 at 0.35 and 0.73 against 0.89 at 0.5. A rough metal reflected up to a fifth less than the trace, which made chrome, gold and lacquered metal read dull. Dielectrics were off by up to 0.08 at grazing angles.

`reflection_dfg(roughness, ndv)` replaces it with a least-squares fit to the tracer's own GGX, integrated over a 39 × 50 grid (roughness 0.05 to 1, n·v 0.02 to 1) with 8,192 samples per point. Its features are `u = 2·roughness − 1` to the fifth power, `s = 2·√(n·v) − 1` to the second, and each of those again times `(1 − n·v)⁵`: 36 coefficients for `A + B` and 36 for `B`, evaluated by Horner's rule in `u`. Over 300 random points off the grid it is within 0.0185 of the integral for a metal (mean 0.0024) and 0.0175 for F0 = 0.04 (mean 0.0013), where the old approximation was within 0.189 and 0.076. `reflect::DFG_FIT` holds the coefficients and `reflect::environment_dfg` is the CPU mirror. `the_environment_brdf_follows_the_tracers_ggx` checks it against an independent 4,096-sample integral, to 0.02 for metals and 0.012 for dielectrics. `the_shader_carries_the_same_fit` checks that the WGSL holds the same numbers. At roughness 0 both forms are 1 for a metal, so mirrors don't change. Every other specular and metallic surface gets brighter by what the old approximation lost, and diffuse takes `1 − F` of the new value.

### Coat reflections and sky visibility

**The coat's own lobe (wave 62).** `reflection_radiance` reads the reflection sources at the coat's roughness for the coat's share and at the base's for the substrate's (`reflection_fallback`, with the same SSR hit and the same local cube weights), as the tracer samples each lobe at its own roughness. Before, both shares read the base's mip, so a glossy coat over a rougher base, or over ink that pulls the base's roughness up, reflected a broad blur of the room: on the repro below, a window half a room away from the coat's mirror direction. A surface without a coat, or with a coat as rough as its base, reads once, as before.

**Sky visibility (wave 62).** The sky cube is the outside, and a surface whose probes see little of it should reflect little of it. Where a baked grid covers the point, the opaque pass scales the sky's share of the reflection (the share no local cube takes: outside every box and across a box's seam fade) by the probes' irradiance along the reflection direction over the sky's SH irradiance along it, clamped to 1. In the open the probes see the sky and the share is kept; in a closed room it falls to about the probes' irradiance over π, the room's own level, with the sky's shape. It is on by default; `ProbeLighting::set_sky_visibility(false)` (and `Frame`'s and `Renderer`'s) turns it off; the switch rides `shade.y` of the probe uniform, so no binding changes. The irradiance along the reflection direction comes from the same pass over the same eight probes as the surface's own (`sample_live_irradiance_toward`): their weights, depth tests and, for a version 2 probe, the block already read, with the six lobes evaluated a second time along the reflection direction and kept as a luminance. A frame without a probe grid, a point outside the grid, a surface with no specular, coat, metal, transmission or film, and a point wholly inside local cubes don't use it, and an instance's `reflection_occlusion` release keeps that much of its sky, as it does for the occlusion. Reflection occlusion's reference takes the sky's share after this scaling, so the two don't darken the same sky twice. It shares the occlusion's blind spot: irradiance can't tell a mirror direction that sees a window from a hemisphere that doesn't, so a glossy part looking out through an opening reads the opening dimmed unless a local cube captures it.

**Why, on a coated top (wave 62).** A product's glossy lacquer box under a lit room with a bright window showed a broad white reflection over its top face, bevel and print live, where the trace kept them dark. The neutral repro is `tests/support/coat_room.rs`: a closed 4 × 2.8 × 4 m room with one 1.6 × 1.2 m window in a wall, a noon sun at 64° through it, and a 52 × 14 × 38 cm box with 3 cm rounded edges on the floor, base (0.66, 0.13, 0.03) at roughness 0.5 under a full coat at 0.06, with dark ink (`Blend::Over`, ink roughness 0.6) printed under the coat on its top. The probes are a 0.3 m grid with a 5 cm local volume over the box, the reflection cube a 128² capture at the room's centre with the room as its box. The camera looks down at 55°, so the coat's mirror direction from the top sees only the ceiling. `examples/coat_room.rs <width> <height> <spp> <frames>` traces it, renders it live in six setups, prints each region's mean luminance against the trace, the median and 95th percentile of the per-pixel ratio and the share of pixels within 0.8–1.25, writes a contact sheet (trace, live, ratio map, difference) to `tmp/coat-room/`, and times the opaque pass. ### Local cubes: distance parallax (wave 68)

**What a product saw.** A coated warm nameplate with dark ink inside a desk-sized baked cube (box 0.9 × 0.5 × 0.55 m, the capture point 5 cm from one face) read far too bright live at noon, and better with local reflections off. The added light was specular and independent of albedo, and the cube's brightest texels were a window.

**What it wasn't.**
- *The face convention.* `a_baked_cube_reads_back_along_each_axis_in_the_live_shader` bakes a black-sky scene with a coloured emitter on each axis and a white one off each axis, and reads it back through a mirror in the live shader: every axis reads its own colour and every off-axis marker reads white, while the same direction flipped in either face axis reads black. Bake and GPU sampling agree.
- *The coat's mip.* Since wave 62 the coat lobe reads every source, the cubes included, at the coat's roughness.
- *Occlusion missing the cube.* Reflection occlusion's reference has carried π times each cube's last mip along `n` since wave 32.

**What it was: the parallax box.** Box parallax treats the box as the geometry the cube saw. A desk-sized box says the walls are 5 to 50 cm away, so a ray leaving the plate toward the room crossed the box's near face a few centimetres from the capture point and was looked up from there at a steep angle: on the neutral repro, straight down at the sunlit desk; on the product's desk, the window. The cube itself was right: the same cube with a room-sized box, or with no parallax at all, agreed with the trace.

**The fix.** The bake now stores, in each cube texel's alpha, the inverse distance from the capture point to the first surface in that direction (a CPU ray cast, 0 for open sky; `docs/bake.md`). The live renderer marches the reflection ray against it instead of the box: from 2 cm out, 9 steps growing 2.5 times each, each one comparing the point's distance from the capture point with the stored distance in its direction, one mip level below 64 px a face; the first step that falls behind a stored surface after one in front is refined by 4 bisections, and counts as a hit only if it lies within 3% (plus the refined interval) of that surface, so a ray that passes behind a nearer object, seen from the capture point, keeps going. A ray that hits nothing reads the cube along its own direction. The box now only says where the cube applies, with its seam fade. The lookup runs once per surface and both lobes share it; a cube without distances (format v1, alpha 1) keeps its box parallax. The inverse is stored rather than the distance because the GGX mips filter alpha with the radiance: averaging distances let one sky texel push a wall beside a window thousands of metres away.

**The repro.** `tests/support/local_cube.rs` builds the neutral scenes in `coat_room`'s room (the window, the sky, the noon sun). The nameplate is a 24 × 8 cm plate tilted back 15° on a desk by the window, coated (base 0.66, 0.13, 0.03 at 0.5 under a full coat at 0.06) with a dark ink core and letters, inside a cube shaped like the product's: the capture point 23 cm above the plate, the box 0.55 × 0.5 × 0.9 m with its near face 5 cm from it. `examples/local_cube.rs <width> <height> <spp> <frames> [nameplate|tunnel|curl|both]` traces it, renders it live in each setup, and prints each region's mean against the trace and the mean 8-bit difference over the plate. **What was tried and dropped.**
- Scaling each cube's share by the probes' irradiance toward the reflection direction over the cube's own, as sky visibility does for the sky: the curl came to 1.04, but a correct room cube went darker, the coat room's print from 0.91 to 0.80 (failing `a_coated_top_under_a_closed_ceiling_agrees_with_the_trace`) and the nameplate's ink core from 0.85 to 0.77. Applied only when reflection occlusion is on, the coat room's print at occlusion 0.5 fell from 0.91 to 0.77; with the occlusion's threshold as a knee it no longer reached the curl.
- Keeping a surface's reflection where the probes see its mirror direction open even though its hemisphere is closed (the curl in wave 49): on a dark glossy card under a 1.5 cm bridge whose grazing mirror ray leaves through the far end, the probes' irradiance toward the mirror direction takes in the window above it, and with no cube the card went from 2.16 to 6.0 of the trace at occlusion 1. An irradiance can't see a direction; a product curl that the occlusion darkens still needs its release until the probes can tell.
- A fixed-point walk instead of the march: on that bridge, where the capture point sees the window and the surface's real ray meets the wall below it, it read the sky, 11 to 13 times the trace. The march reads 1.49 under the bridge and 0.45 in front of it (the bridge's edge, seen from the capture point, is taken for a surface), against the box's 1.48 and 1.77; `a_card_under_a_bridge_reads_no_worse_with_distances_than_with_the_box` holds the card's error with distances to the box's.

## Text, images and sampling

`crates/text`'s interface is the intended source of MSDF and exact-coverage glyphs; it is presently a placeholder, so this section defines its live sampling contract rather than claiming it already works. Shape once in app layout space. Put screen-space UI text through an exact-coverage atlas rendered after TAA (or through a history-aware text pass if it must be in the 3D image). Use MSDF for perspective text and moderate scale changes, with derivative-based screen-pixel range and a coverage fallback at very small sizes. Generate glyph atlas mip levels with distance-field-aware filtering; ordinary averaging of signed distances produces wrong edges. Use gutter padding and per-glyph UV bounds to prevent neighbors bleeding. At 4K evaluate glyphs at actual output pixel density, not twice a lower-resolution layout.

Images, ink, wood and icons have color-correct mip chains: filter color in linear light, premultiply alpha before reduction, preserve alpha coverage for cutout masks, and encode sampled color/normal data in their proper formats. Content slots and material maps sample at 16× anisotropy with a −0.25 LOD bias, both measured (see [Crisp text, sharpening and texture filtering](#crisp-text-sharpening-and-texture-filtering-wave-69)). Keep UV gradients across the scroll deformation, then select mips from screen derivatives and clamp only to prevent atlas bleed. An 8192×4096 ink atlas and plate, decal and video textures need this path; fixed or level-zero sampling is insufficient at glancing 4K views. Decode video at source resolution and display it sharply without claiming detail absent from a 720p source. Small fixed icon and text atlases give way to the shared loader and glyph path, keeping their object-space attachment. A 4K reference view of a close sheet should resolve roughly 5,800 texels/m; generate higher-resolution ink pages or vector-backed glyph layers where roughly 4,400 texels/m undersamples.

### The shared glyph atlas

`TextEngine::render_spans` still builds one atlas per paragraph. Each distinct set of glyphs gets its own atlas, kept until the frame stops using it: `end_frame` now drops atlases and rasters that `begin_frame` saw go unused, as it already did for laid-out blocks. Text that changes every frame takes the shared path instead:

- **Cells per glyph.** `place_spans` (shaped text) and `place_number` (numbers) return `Placed`: `PageQuad`s that each name a page and carry the usual `RichGlyphQuad`. A cell is keyed per glyph (font, glyph, weight and optical-size coordinates, fake italic, representation, field size). Coverage cells also key their display size and quarter-pixel bin, as `render_spans` does. MSDF cells are size-free, so one cell serves every size up to `FINE_MSDF_FROM` (120 device pixels) and another every size above it.
- **Pages.** Pages are square (`DEFAULT_ATLAS_PAGE`, 1024; `set_atlas_page` changes it and clears the atlas) and belong to one face and one kind: coverage (R8, four mips), MSDF (RGBA8 on the GPU, one level, since the MSDF branch reads level 0) or colour. Cells sit on shelves in 8-texel units with the 8-texel gutter, so a cell's mips never touch a neighbour's.
- **The budget.** `set_atlas_budget(bytes)` caps the GPU bytes of all pages together, every mip level counted (default `DEFAULT_ATLAS_BUDGET`, 32 MiB). A new page is opened only while the total stays within the budget. Otherwise the least recently used cells are evicted until the glyph fits, and a page left empty is dropped. Cells used since `begin_frame` are never evicted, so quads already handed out this frame stay valid. If the frame's own glyphs do not fit, placing fails with `Error::AtlasTooLarge`. A caller on the shared path calls `begin_frame` and `end_frame` every frame.
- **Uploads.** `take_atlas_changes` returns the pages created and dropped and one `CellUpload` per new cell and mip level: the bytes of that cell's slot and nothing else. On the GPU, `GlyphPages::sync` creates blank page textures (`GpuAtlas::blank`) and writes each cell with `GpuAtlas::write_cells`. That returns `UploadRegion`s, which now carry the columns as well as the rows (`GpuAtlas::update` reports full-width rows as before). `page_quads` groups a frame's quads by page, one `QuadDraw` per page. Colour pages stay CPU-side for `print_spans`, as colour atlases already did.
- **The field.** Shared MSDF cells use a sharp field: pseudo-distances to the nearest edge per channel, so the median keeps convex corners instead of rounding them as a plain distance field does. Edge colouring never gives the two edges at a corner the same colour, and clashing texels are flattened to their median. Both fields take the inside from the nonzero winding rule and ignore edge pieces buried inside another contour. Variable fonts keep overlapping contours (in DM Sans `#$&+@BQRefg`), and the even-odd test used before drew a seam through them, such as the bar of the "e". For glyphs without overlaps (all of IBM Plex Mono and EB Garamond) `render_spans` writes the same bytes as before.

### Numbers without shaping

`place_number(span, chars, anchor, placement)` lays out labels made only of the characters in `chars`, the glyph set the caller passes (a game's digits and separators; the tests use `0123456789,.e+-Na`), with no cosmic-text shaping. The set is part of the face's cache key, so one face can serve several sets. The first use of a face, size and scale shapes two buffers once. One holds the set's characters on their own lines, which gives each glyph, its advance and its baseline. The other is a sequence of n² + 1 characters (290 for 17) holding every ordered pair once, which gives the kerning of each pair. Both are shaped with `tnum`. A font without `tnum` (DM Sans has none) gets tabular digits anyway: every ASCII digit in the set takes the widest digit's advance, centred in it, and digit pairs drop their kerning. After that a label is a walk over cached advances and kerns, cells come from the shared atlas, and the anchor moves the label by its width. A label with any other character goes through shaping with `tnum` on. `atlas_stats()` counts shapings, fast labels, rasters, evictions and uploaded bytes. Over 1,000 frames of 80 labels changing every frame (`eighty_labels_change_every_frame_for_a_thousand_frames_within_the_budget`), nothing is shaped, rasterized or uploaded once the first frame has seen each glyph. At 4K, 80 changing labels draw in 0.076 ms (`eighty_changing_numbers_at_4k_upload_nothing_once_warm`).

### Baked detail maps

Static parts can trade their procedural layers for baked maps, one crop per part packed onto atlas pages (`docs/bake.md`, "Procedural detail maps"). `Renderer::set_detail(&atlas)` builds the material map arrays at group 3 from the pages: base colour at binding 0, normal at 1, roughness at 2, and at 3 the metal array, which holds only the lacquer's and the detail noise tiles. A device with `TEXTURE_COMPRESSION_BC` gets the pages as stored, BC7 sRGB, BC5 and BC4 with their five mip levels. Any other device gets them decoded to RGBA8 on the CPU, so the floor does not change. The binding numbers stay the same and nothing is added.

`select_baked_detail(&mut material, k)` points a material at part `k` of the atlas. `MapTextures::material` turns that into the map material at binding 5: all three maps use the part's page as their layer, and the map material's last row, `atlas`, holds the part's `scale.xy` and `offset.xy`. `seeds.w` says how the maps are read: 0 procedural, 1 a full-tile map, 2 an atlas with RGB normals, 3 an atlas with BC5 normals, whose z the shader rebuilds as √(1 − x² − y²). `map_evaluate` samples at `uv × atlas.xy + atlas.zw`, so a part looks as it did with its full-tile map. Under `set_detail`, a material that is not baked detail reads no maps. `set_maps` with full-tile images still works as before.

A part baked through `TEXCOORD_1` samples its atlas through it. `MeshData::uvs1` takes the second set (`None` for every mesh without one). The vertex keeps its 64 bytes: the second set rides in two lanes that were padding, `uv_alpha.w` (u) and `normal.w` (v), and a mesh without it writes 0 there as before. The opaque vertex stage passes `normal` on as a `vec4f`, so the set reaches the fragment in the two interpolants that already carry `normal` and `uv_alpha`, with no new attribute, varying, buffer or pipeline layout. That is cheaper than either alternative the format allowed: a second vertex buffer adds a binding and an 8-byte fetch per vertex for the meshes that have the set, and a second vertex format (80 bytes) needs its own pipelines and still adds the bytes; here every mesh fetches exactly what it fetched before, and the set costs nothing where it is unused. `MapTextures::material` writes the part's set into the map material's `packed.y`, a lane no other path reads for baked detail, and `map_evaluate_with` samples the atlas at `uv1 × atlas.xy + atlas.zw` when `packed.y` is 1. Everything else in the material (ageing, content, procedural layers on unbaked parts) keeps `TEXCOORD_0`.

The choice is specialized like the rest of the opaque pass: `OpaqueFeatures::uv1` is on only for a part whose map material says set 1, and the `use_uv1` override compiles the branch out of every other part's pipeline. Without `TEXCOORD_1` the frame is the same, byte for byte: `a_plain_scene_draws_the_same_bytes_with_its_unused_features_compiled_out` lists `uv1` among the features compiled out, and `a_quad_samples_detail_baked_through_its_second_uv_set_through_that_set` draws a quad whose second set mirrors the first over a two-colour atlas: baked through set 0 it shows the halves one way round, through set 1 the other, the general pipeline matches the specialized one, and a mesh with a second set baked through set 0 draws the same bytes as one without it. `trace::stage::Mesh` does not carry the set: the path tracer evaluates procedural detail directly and never samples a baked atlas.

Measured against full-tile maps by `maps::tests::cropped_compressed_detail_samples_like_the_full_tile`: three parts at 0 to about 3.8 mip levels. The crop matches to 0.07/255 or better. BC7, BC5 and BC4 against uncompressed give 41 to 67 dB, and the CPU-decoded path is within 1.74/255 of the GPU's BC decode.

## Anti-aliasing and finish

Use native-resolution TAA with an 8-frame subpixel jitter cycle, object/camera motion vectors, depth and normal/ID rejection, exposure-aware history rescaling, and neighborhood clipping in a perceptual or YCoCg space. Limit accumulation for moving glyphs, foliage, particles and specular highlights; use reactive masks for transparency, emission and changing print. Reset or reproject correctly on camera cuts, fixture changes, resize, daylight jumps and scroll uncovering. Apply a mild, bounded sharpening filter only after validation; it cannot rescue undersampled assets. Keep UI text and selection indicators after TAA to avoid ghosting. An app with its own temporal accumulation still needs explicit reprojection and disocclusion rules for raster motion.

Compare 4× MSAA at native 4K in the prototype. It helps geometric edges but multiplies color/depth bandwidth and does not resolve texture shimmer, subpixel foliage or reflections. TAA is the default if its history handling passes scrolling text, moving chrome and thin-line tests; targeted alpha-to-coverage or 2×/4× MSAA on a limited pass may be retained if a specific edge needs it. Never use TAA as a blur that hides low-detail geometry.

One shared finish works in scene-linear HDR: exposure, bloom threshold and multi-level downsample/upsample at half or quarter resolution, a single agreed tone curve, then product grade and SDR sRGB/BT.709 encoding with dither. The material and lighting output must be compared **before** grade as well as after. Each product's look (a warm grade, a warmth and vignette, a brighter filmic response) is a chain the product builds from the shared passes, with regression images in its own repository. An 80-tap-per-pixel finish bloom is replaced by the mip pyramid. Keep offline still/clip color metadata consistent; do not silently double-encode gamma.

`pfx_post::gpu::GpuChain` plans a chain before it builds pipelines. It drops identity passes, then merges every run of per-pixel passes into one `Pass::Grade`, which is a single full-screen compute pass. The per-pixel passes are exposure, black, saturation, contrast, warmth, tone, vignette, grain, dither, encode, bw, noir, sepia, posterize, flicker, scanlines, aperture, duotone and gradient map. A run can also take one bloom, provided only exposures come before it. The bloom keeps its own extract and blur stages at its reduced resolution, its extract scales by the run's exposure, and its composite goes into the grade pass. Passes that read neighbours or geometry (cel, outline, kuwahara, halation, distortion and the like) end a run.

The grade shader is generated from the same `grade_*` WGSL functions the standalone passes call. It rounds through f16 after every step, as each texture store between passes did before, so fused and unfused chains give the same pixels. The renderer's runtime exposure multiplies the run's first exposure and the bloom extract's scale. Stage labels read `post <group>:<stage> <name>`, for example `post 0:3 grade add`, and the renderer sums them under `post`.

### Crisp text, sharpening and texture filtering (wave 69)

**Text out of the history.** Surface text (`Text::surface` and `surface_quads`, lit or not, deformed or not) now draws after the TAA resolve, into the crisp display image that the depth of field, the exposure meter and the post chain then read. It never enters the TAA history, so it neither softens nor ghosts. It keeps its depth test against the frame's depth buffer, so geometry in front still hides it, and its edges stay anti-aliased by their own MSDF coverage. The caller's text matrices were never jittered, so text drawn after the resolve sits still on the jittered frame. The resolved image itself stays as it was: the crisp display image is a copy of it (`crisp copy`) or its sharpened version, made only when the frame has surface text with glyphs or sharpening is on. `Renderer::set_text_after_taa(false)` puts text back before the resolve. With that and sharpening off, the frame runs exactly the passes it ran before, and a frame without surface text and without sharpening is the same bytes either way (`the_crisp_passes_run_only_when_asked`). Overlay text was already drawn after the post chain and is unchanged. The geometry under text is still jittered, so where a mesh hides text its edge follows that mesh's anti-aliasing. A reprojected or partly redrawn frame has to take its text from the display image or redraw it, since the history holds none.

Still text was already about as sharp in the history, because unjittered glyphs over a jittered background repeat the same pixels every frame. A drifting camera reprojects the history bilinearly every frame, and that blurs text to 2.42 px and loses half the clean edges. Drawn after the resolve, drifting text matches still text and sits 3% above the trace's lettering. Sharpening can't bring softened text back: at full strength it only moves the median by 2%.

**Sharpening.** `Renderer::set_sharpen(strength)` (0 to 1, default 0, off) runs a contrast-adaptive sharpening pass on the resolved image, AMD's CAS without scaling: each channel's weight comes from the 3×3 neighbourhood's minimum and maximum, so flat regions and strong edges are left alone and soft detail is lifted. Its negative lobe is `strength / mix(8, 5, strength)` (5 is CAS's strongest). It works on HDR values squashed by `c / (1 + max(c))` after the exposure and unsquashes after, so highlights keep their level, and alpha passes through. Text is drawn after it, so glyphs are never sharpened. At 0 the pass doesn't run and frames are today's bytes. `crisp::sharpen` is the CPU reference, and the GPU matches it to 0.1% relative (`the_sharpen_pass_matches_its_cpu_reference`).

**Content and detail filtering.** Content slots and material maps (full-tile and baked detail atlases) sample at 16× anisotropy, up from 8×, with a −0.25 LOD bias (`maps::TextureFilter`, `CONTENT_LOD_BIAS` and `DETAIL_LOD_BIAS`, set with `Renderer::set_texture_filter` or `Frame::set_texture_filter`; `TextureFilter::UNBIASED` is the old 8× unbiased filter). The content bias scales the gradients `content_texel` already passes by `exp2(bias)`, from the map material's `content_extra.y`. The map reads become `textureSampleGrad` with the tile coordinate's screen derivatives scaled the same way, from `content_extra.z`. Neither needs a new binding. A first version read the maps through `textureSampleBias`, and on RADV with anisotropic filtering that bias barely moved the image (RMS 0.0295 at 0, 0.0289 at −1, flicker unchanged), while scaled gradients behave exactly as the content's do. The noise tiles share the map sampler, so they get 16× too, without the bias.

The bias was picked by measurement (`the_lod_bias_trades_sharpness_against_shimmer`): a printed page (1144×2515 texels of text) on a 1.2 × 1.6 m floor, seen at a glancing angle at 960×540, against the same view rendered at 3840×2160 and averaged 4×4 as the reference. Sharpness is the mean gradient magnitude over the floor divided by the reference's, and "far" is the 120 rows nearest the horizon, where minification is strongest. Flicker is the mean absolute second difference over time of each pixel: over 8 frames with only the jitter (still), and over 24 frames of a slow dolly (moving).

| Anisotropy, bias, sharpen | Sharpness (far) | RMS against 4× | Flicker still | Flicker moving (far) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 8×, 0, 0 (before) | 0.921 (0.889) | 0.0315 | 0.00641 | 0.00940 (0.00998) |
| 16×, 0, 0 | 0.927 (0.899) | 0.0295 | 0.00647 | 0.00950 (0.01016) |
| **16×, −0.25, 0 (default)** | **0.931 (0.903)** | **0.0272** | **0.00717** | **0.01028 (0.01157)** |
| 16×, −0.5, 0 | 0.932 (0.901) | 0.0273 | 0.00793 | 0.01109 (0.01325) |
| 16×, −0.75, 0 | 0.932 (0.898) | 0.0288 | 0.00864 | 0.01187 (0.01491) |
| 16×, −1, 0 | 0.930 (0.893) | 0.0309 | 0.00929 | 0.01254 (0.01641) |
| 16×, −0.25, 0.25 | 0.953 (0.930) | 0.0242 | 0.00769 | 0.01101 (0.01259) |
| 16×, −0.25, 0.5 | 0.996 (0.980) | 0.0227 | 0.00856 | 0.01222 (0.01428) |
| 16×, −0.25, 1 | 1.387 (1.439) | 0.0803 | 0.01485 | 0.02277 (0.02823) |

TAA already averages the jittered samples, so a negative bias buys little sharpness here and mostly adds shimmer. −0.25 gives the image closest to the reference, at 11% more flicker still and 9% more moving. Past −0.5 the error grows again. 16× over 8× moves the far rows from 0.889 to 0.899 and the error by 6%, for 1% more flicker. Sharpening at 0.5 brings the page to the reference's sharpness, with the lowest error of all, for 19% more flicker than the default. At 1 it overshoots. Products that want it set 0.25 to 0.5. The same page as a full-tile base map gives the same figures row for row, so the detail bias is −0.25 too.

**Cost at 4K** (3840×2160, RX 9060 XT, medians over 48 to 120 frames): surface text after TAA costs what it did before, 0.007 ms for the page above. The crisp copy costs 0.164 ms whenever a frame has surface text and no sharpening. The sharpen pass costs 0.34 ms and replaces the copy. A version that squashed a shared 18 × 18 tile once per workgroup measured 0.357 ms, so the plain nine-tap pass stayed. The TAA resolve is unchanged (0.98 ms still, 1.04 ms drifting). 16× anisotropy and the bias cost nothing measurable in the opaque pass with the floor filling the frame: 0.856 ms at 8× unbiased, 0.856 ms at 16×, 0.847 ms at 16× with a −0.5 bias.

### Tape glitch

`pfx_post::Tape` is the colour glitch of a VHS tape that is fast-forwarding or rewinding. It runs as one full-screen pass (`Pass::Tape`) after tone mapping and the grade, on the display-referred image:

- **Channel split:** each channel keeps part of itself in place and moves the rest as a sharp copy. Red sends 59% of itself `0.0034 × width` to one side and blue 47% of itself `0.0039 × width` to the other; green stays almost whole, with a 6% trace beside the red copy and 2% beside the blue. In rewind red goes left and blue right, so glyphs split into red, green and blue images with magenta and yellow where they overlap, and "1 kg" reads "11 kg". Forward mirrors it. No channel is blurred. On a light ground the same split shows dark glyphs as magenta with cyan and yellow copies.
- **Lift:** blacks lift to about 19/255; saturation and sharpness are kept.
- **Texture:** faint grain on luma and chroma, and faint scanlines.
- **Tracking bands:** three bands roll through the frame. In a still they show only as a slight sideways bend of their rows, a sub-pixel jitter and occasional faint noise. Forward rolls them down and rewind rolls them up.
- **Optional:** `echo` adds two fainter copies trailing against the travel, mixed in rather than added. `smear` softens by half a pixel, bleeds red and blue around their copies and lowers saturation. Both are off by default.

The split weights and offsets were fitted by least squares, in linear light, to a reference pair (`tape-before.jpeg` → `tape-after.jpeg`, 1600 px wide) over the formula, axis-label and grid regions. The fit found red at 0.42 in place and 0.60 at 5.5 px left, green at 0.90 in place, and blue at 0.43 in place and 0.48 at 6.3 px right. The reference's black measures 19.3/255. At strength 1 the pass reads three horizontal bilinear samples per pixel through the chain's linear sampler: in place, at the red copy and at the blue copy. The echo adds two more and the smear two more. The stage uses `Aux::StudioBlur`'s layout, a filterable source and a sampler at binding 3. The lift, grain and bands work on luma and chroma in a gamma-2 space. The pass adds no binding to the live renderer's group 3. A first design kept each row segment in workgroup memory; it cost 0.5–3 ms at 4K on this GPU, mostly in instruction issue and occupancy, so the sampler design replaced it.

| Field | Range | Default | Effect at 1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `strength` | 0 to 1 | — | 0 is off and byte-identical to the chain without the tape |
| `direction` | `Forward`, `Rewind` | — | the sides of the red and blue copies and of the echo, and the bands' roll |
| `split` | ≥ 0 | 1 | red's copy `0.0034 × width` and blue's `0.0039 × width` away (6.5 and 7.5 px at 1080p, 13 and 15 px at 4K) |
| `echo` | 0 to 2 | 0 | copies at `0.007` and `0.016 × width`, mixed in at 25% and 10% |
| `wash` | 0 to 1 | 1 | black lifts to 0.07 in gamma 2, about 19/255 |
| `smear` | 0 to 1 | 0 | a half-pixel softening, red and blue bleed over ±`0.75 × width / 1920` pixels, saturation ×0.78 |
| `grain` | ≥ 0 | 1 | grain of ±0.008 (triangular) and 4% scanlines, two rows per period at 1080p and four at 4K |
| `bands` | ≥ 0 | 1 | the bands, bending their rows by up to `0.0025 × width` |

Each amount is relative: it applies at `strength × amount`, so a ramp of `strength` keeps the look's shape. Every offset scales with the image width, so 1080p and 4K look alike. Time and seed come from the chain's `frame` and `seed`, the renderer's `round(Scene.time × 60)` and `Scene.seed`, so the same frame and seed give the same bytes and the pass never reads the wall clock. `Tape::split_pixels` (red and blue), `echo_pixels`, `band_centres` and `envelope` give the same numbers on the CPU, and `tape::apply` is the CPU reference.

A finish turns it on through the preset path: in a post table, `tape = 0.8` is forward at 0.8, `tape = "rewind"` is rewind at 1, and the table form sets everything:

```toml
style = "film"
tape = { strength = 0.8, direction = "rewind", grain = 0.5 }
```

In code, `Chain::set_tape(Tape::rewind(0.8))` replaces the chain's tape or adds one before its trailing encode. To ramp it per frame around a seek, `Renderer::set_tape(Some(tape))` sets it on the live renderer's chain on every frame, including through style changes, until `set_tape(None)` hands back the finish's own tape. Outside the renderer, `GpuChain::set_tape` does the same. Once the tape's pipelines exist, changing the tape rebuilds nothing.

`GpuChain` plans a chain that holds a tape twice. One plan is the chain without the tape, exactly as before. The other has the tape after the grade, and when the chain ends in an encode, the tape pass writes the encoded image itself, so the grade drops its encode and no extra pass runs. While the tape is off the first plan runs, so strength 0 gives the same bytes as before. The second plan's pipelines are built when the chain is created with a tape, or on the first `set_tape` with a strength above 0.

Measured on 2026-10-04 at 3840×2160 on the RX 9060 XT under `pgpu`, as medians over 20 frames (`tape::tests::benchmark_4k`): the tape pass alone takes 0.26 ms, and a warm grade as the renderer runs it takes 0.43 ms with a rewind at 1 (grade 0.15 ms, tape with encode 0.29 ms) against 0.15 ms without.

## `pfx bake` artifact contract

`pfx bake` implements this command. Proposed invocation: `pfx bake --app <app> --scene <scene> --rev <tag-or-rev> --fixtures <set> --seed <n> --out tmp/bakes`, using the same adapter/scene snapshot as live and offline stills. The product supplies content through its own adapter; the engine does not read a fixture repository or write inside an app repository. Bake scratch and outputs live under the caller's `tmp/`; app build artifacts and reusable tools stay in the machine cache. The product's owning session chooses which verified bake files to package as immutable, content-hashed assets and loads them through the shared asset loader. The live app never calls `pfx bake` at startup.

| Output | Disk format and estimated size | Runtime use |
|---|---|---|
| Directional lightmap pages | KTX2 BC6H for HDR irradiance, mipmapped; one 4096² page about 21.3 MiB including mips, plus UV chart metadata | Static diffuse indirect; fallback RGBA16F about 170.7 MiB/page if BC6H unsupported |
| Probe brick/volume | Versioned binary: positions, six RGB16F lobes and visibility; 5,400 probes about 0.3–0.6 MiB depending visibility/alignment, before compression | Movable/deforming indirect light |
| Reflection probes | KTX2 BC6H prefiltered cubemaps; one 256² cube about 0.5 MiB with mips | Rough/clear reflection fallback |
| Prerendered prop layers | KTX2 BC7/sRGB for albedo, BC5 normals, optional BC6H emission, mesh LODs and atlas metadata; e.g. 2048² BC7 about 5.3 MiB with mips | Static high-detail props without per-frame trace |
| Manifest | JSON schema/version, relative paths, SHA-256/bytes, color space, coordinate/UV conventions, bake anchor, sample count, variance, source IDs and hashes | Validation and deterministic loading |

Numbers use block-compression byte rates and a 4/3 mip overhead; they exclude container headers and compression variability. A first per-scene shipping target is **under 256 MiB** for all daylight anchors, static lighting, reflection probes and prerendered props; content can require a different cap after memory/disk measurements. Reserve at most 1 GiB VRAM for resident bakes in the 16 GiB card, stream only needed scenes/anchors, and upload outside the critical frame path. If BC texture features or KTX2 transcoding are unavailable, ship a tested uncompressed fallback only when its size is acceptable, or fail the preset validation explicitly. The manifest records format alternatives so the loader never guesses.

Hash canonical scene geometry, materials, static lights, sky, fixture contents, shader/baker version, recipe, anchor time and seed. Sort objects/assets by stable ID before hashing; use fixed sample sequences and fixed summation order where possible. Write to a temporary path inside the caller's `tmp/`, checksum, then atomically rename. Same inputs on the same GPU and driver should produce the same bytes or, where floating-point compression makes bit identity impossible, the manifest must state a bounded numerical tolerance and record the GPU/driver. No wall clock or system randomness enters the bake. Validate hashes before loading, reject stale scene/material versions, and keep the previous verified package until a complete replacement is present. This design requests a bake-specific manifest stanza before the command ships, rather than changing that shared document here.

## Shippable migration order

Every stage leaves each existing app running and can be pinned independently. Keep the old live renderer behind an app-controlled fallback until a like-for-like native-4K comparison passes; the product owns the toggle and release decision. Share one scene snapshot, one material meaning and one finish color contract so offline shots continue to depict the actual app revision.

At each stage, capture the same fixture state, camera, time, age and seed in old live, new live and offline reference; compare crops and short motion sequences at native 4K. A product may ship its raster path while another stays on its older renderer. The common engine API and bake versions remain backward-compatible for already pinned products, with an explicit migration when they change.

## Room haze from one amount

`RoomHaze::from_amount(lo, hi, amount)` is one default model for the room haze, for recipes that give a single number: `amount` is clamped to 0 to 1, where 0 is no haze and 1 a clearly hazy room, and `lo` and `hi` are the haze box. Fog is `amount / 6`, smoke `20 × amount`, mist `0.2 × amount` and floor `2 × amount`; the rest comes from `RoomHaze::new(lo, hi)`: phase 0.3, back 0.5, reach 3, gold neutral and the ambient a neutral grey of 0.03 (`HAZE_AMBIENT_GREY`). Every field is public, so a product sets its own phase, back, reach and gold after the call.

Group 2 holds what moves vertices, so the prepass, opaque, caster and deformed text pipelines all bind the same deformer group. Group 3 holds what shades a surface once its geometry is known; it is rebuilt each frame because the SSR history alternates. Blossom and leaf cards keep groups 0, 1 and 3 and put the canopy's own card data at group 2. Passes that are not opaque surfaces (sky, glass, impostors, SSR, effects, TAA, post) keep their own layouts. Content layers blend as `Over`, `Multiply` or `Emission`; `ContentLayer::from_kind(content, clearcoat, slot, &look)` maps a material's content kind onto a blend with the gains, ink roughness and emboss of the caller's `ContentLook` (neutral by default: no roughness change, no emboss, unit gains). Base arrays are `Rgba8UnormSrgb` with premultiplied linear-light mips, and `GpuMapMaterial` is 464 bytes: each noise layer carries its kind row and four parameter rows, twelve values the layer's kind defines and four the packer derives (a plank's reciprocal width and seam span, a scratch's stretch, the ring fade rate, a coat's fine scale and tile match). Group 3 also holds the content slots: bindings 18 to 21 are four `texture_2d` content slots and 22 their clamped, mipped, 8× anisotropic sampler, owned by `maps`. `Instance::shadow_only` keeps an instance in the cascades, light faces and transmission and out of every visible pass; `Instance::two_sided` draws both faces with the normal turned to the viewer; `InstanceSurface::face` (`ContentFace::{Both, Front, Back}`, default `Both`) says which face shows its content layer, and the blend is skipped, not mirrored, on the other, so a printed strip rolled in place keeps its outside blank. The opaque pass has an `opaque_lit_main` variant that runs only while a local light is lit or sun transmission is set; without them the old shader runs and frames are byte-identical. Per-instance clip planes (`clip0`, `clip1`) and content placement (`content_uv`, `content_crop`) come from `Frame::set_surfaces`, indexed like `Scene.instances`; clipped shadow casters draw through a second caster pipeline with a fragment stage, and casters cut by their content (`InstanceSurface::cutout`) through a third that also binds group 3 for the content slots.
The opaque pass compiles one pipeline per set of features a batch uses (`frame::OpaqueFeatures`: the sun's shadow, the soft sun's blocker search and filter, cascade blending, probes, local reflection cubes, SSR, the canopy, contact, authored ambient, caustics, content layers, material maps, procedural detail, ageing, the second UV set, content cutouts, the lacquer bump and six detail kinds). Each feature is a WGSL `override` that defaults to on, except `fine_noise`, `procedural_lacquer` and `procedural_detail`, which no instance asks for: forced on, `fine_noise` evaluates every noise octave under the pixel footprint at full cost, as the shader did before octaves faded, `procedural_lacquer` hashes the lacquer bump as before the tile, and `procedural_detail` hashes the tiled detail kinds as before their tiles; `lacquer` is on only for parts whose bump is lacquer's, and `scratch`, `grime`, `wood`, `wall`, `fibre` and `crinkle` only for parts with a layer of that kind, so each kind compiles only into the parts that use it; a feature is compiled out of a batch only where the frame or the batch's instances leave it unused (no soft sun, a zero cascade blend, no probe grid, no local cubes, no SSR, no canopy; no instance flag, no content slot, no map index, no noise layer with an amplitude, no age), and its compiled-out path is the same arithmetic the runtime branch takes when unused, so those frames are byte-identical (`tests/opaque_features.rs`). A pipeline is keyed by `frame::OpaqueKey` (the features, the lit variant and whether the batch is two-sided, which picks back-face culling or none), made on first use and kept. `Frame::warm_opaque` makes both sides of one set ahead of time, and `Renderer::warm_opaque` forwards to the frame, because `Renderer::frame` only lends it; `Renderer::warm` makes a whole scene's on worker threads (see Cold start below). A shadow-only batch never draws in the opaque pass, so the frame compiles no opaque pipeline for it. `Frame::opaque_drawn` lists the sets the last frame drew with, and `Frame::set_opaque_on` and `set_opaque_off` force features on or off for measurement. The general shader (every feature) is about 73,000 instructions and 96 VGPRs on RADV; a plain part's is about 3,000 instructions and 60 VGPRs, and procedural detail alone is about 60,000 of those instructions. Exact early outs skip the sun's shadow, canopy and caustic where `N·L` is zero or the sun is already shadowed, and every reflection sample whose weight is zero. The soft sun's knobs are on `shadow::Quality`: `blocker_taps` (8) and `filter_taps` (12), `nearest_blocker` (the nearest cluster of blockers sets the penumbra when it holds at least half of them, so a near contact stays dark beside far casters), `umbra_skip` (a fully blocked search skips the filter), `blend_dither` (the blend band takes one cascade, picked per pixel, instead of two) and `blocker_gathers` (0; four gathers read sixteen depths in four fetches but darken a grazing receiver by up to 1%). `ProbeLighting::set_corner_floor` (default `probes::PROBE_CORNER_FLOOR`, 0.05) fades out trilinear probe corners whose weight is below the floor, about 42% of the corner work; 0 reproduces the old blend exactly.

Many images, one content slot: a sprite-heavy 2D scene packs its images into one slot with `content_atlas::ContentAtlas` instead of asking for more slots. There are four slots because the floor is 18 sampled textures per stage (`docs/gpu-floor.md`): lit tree cards and lit text bind 18 in the fragment stage, four of them the content slots, and every further slot would raise the floor above 18 for every device. Each instance already picks a rectangle of its slot, so an atlas needs no new binding: `ContentAtlas::surface(id)` returns the `InstanceSurface` (`uv_offset`, `uv_scale`, `crop`) that puts image `id` on a quad whose UVs run 0 to 1. Images pack on shelves, best-fit by shelf height, so the same images in the same order always land in the same places; `add_all` packs a batch tallest first and returns ids in input order, and `add` puts a later image into free space without moving one already placed, until `AtlasError::Full` says which cell no longer fits. Every image sits in a cell with a gutter of `2^level` texels on each side, filled with the image's own edge texels, and its size and origin are rounded to multiples of `2^level`, so no mip block at levels 0 to `level` mixes in a neighbour: the default level 4 (`DEFAULT_BLEED_LEVEL`) keeps neighbours out down to 16× minification, and a smaller level wastes less space on small images. `upload` writes only the cells added since the last upload and rebuilds the slot's mips only over those cells, in one submission. For each new cell it rebuilds, at every mip level, the rectangle that reads the level above's changed texels (`maps::mip_regions`: the cell's footprint, gutters included, widened by one texel each side for the bilinear reads and rounded out), all level 1 rectangles before any level 2, so a level never reads a texel that is about to change; a rectangle is one scissored pass with the load op `Load` for `Srgb8` and one dispatch over an origin-offset kernel for `Linear16`, using the same sampling and the same texel-centre arithmetic as the full rebuild, so the result equals `Frame::regenerate_content` byte for byte (a test reads every level back after both). `upload_paced(frame, slot, &mut pacer, between)` sends the same work through a `Pacer` (`pfx_gpu::pace`) as `Work::Dispatches`, each cell write and each rectangle being one dispatch, so the Pacer sizes each submission to its target (2 to 6 ms) and learns from the label `content atlas mips`; the cell writes go in just before the submission that follows them, so a first submission does not carry 64 uploads at once, and `between` is the usual per-submission hook (`Turns::add` under pgpu). It returns the cells written and the `Stats` (submission count, longest and median in ms). Paced and unpaced uploads give identical levels. `write` and `copy` still rebuild the whole chain. The side is at most the device's `max_texture_dimension_2d` and the floor's 16384. With level 4 a 512² card takes a 544² cell, so 225 cards fit at 8192² (341.3 MiB with mips as `Srgb8`, 682.7 MiB as `Linear16`) and 900 at 16384² (1365.3 MiB, 2730.7 MiB); with level 0 it takes 514², 225 and 961. Measured on 2026-10-04 under `pgpu`: 64 cards of 64² through one 1024² slot, 32 uploaded then 32 added, each show their own image within 1/255 at mip 0, and 64 cards of 512² through one 8192² slot, minified 16×, stay within 1/255 of their own image where a gutter of one texel bleeds visibly.

`Instance::casts_shadow` (default `true` in `Instance::new`) is the counterpart of `shadow_only`: with `false` the instance draws in every visible pass and stays out of the sun cascades (and their static cache key), sun transmission and local light shadows. An instance with `shadow_only` set and `casts_shadow` false draws nothing, and `render` accepts it.

Glass casts its own tinted shadow, from the sun and from every shadowed local light, when `glass::Surface::casts_shadow` is set; with it false, frames are unchanged. A casting surface draws into the sun transmission map (group 1 binding 4) in the same pass and depth test as transmissive instances, so the nearest caster's tint wins, and stays out of the depth cascades, as a transmissive instance does. Its tint is `glass::cast_tint(material)`: transmission × base, or transmission × `beer(thickness, base, absorption)` when thickness and absorption are both above zero, each channel clamped to 0–1, which is what a product computes for its shadow-only copies. A surface with `tinted` mixes toward `cast_tint(tinted)` by the vertex look's tint where the look's flag is set, so the liquid grid casts each texel's tint from one surface, and discards where the look's cut is below zero, as the glass front pass does; a `liquid` surface is displaced by the fluid and ripple heights as the glass pass displaces it. The cast writes instance 0 into the map, so no opaque receiver skips it as its own shadow. While any surface casts, the transmission map redraws every frame, because a GPU-rewritten liquid grid changes without the CPU knowing. `Renderer::render` builds the casts from `Effects::glass` before the cascades (`glass::Pass::prepare_casts`, then `draw_casts` from `Frame::encode_with_casts`'s transmission pass). This replaces the shadow-only `Instance` copy with `set_transmission` that an app keeps per glass part: `renderer::tests::glass_casts_the_shadow_its_shadow_only_copy_cast` renders a tinted slab over a floor both ways and the frames do not differ in a single byte (floor under the slab at 0.775, 0.850, 0.982 of the open floor either way). With the camera still, the copy's transmission is cached and costs nothing while the glass redraws it every frame, 0.105 to 0.119 ms for a 1024² map per cascade at a 4096² atlas (`glass_casting_costs_about_what_its_copy_costs`, two runs under `pgpu`); with the copy moving, as GPU-rewritten shadow grids are, the copy costs 0.118 ms of transmission and 0.007 ms of cascades, so the glass costs what a moving copy costs, and saves its upload and the pass that rewrites it.

`glass::Surface::shadow_only` is glass's counterpart of `Instance::shadow_only`: the surface stays out of the glass pass (front, back, compose and ids) and keeps casting its tinted shadow when `casts_shadow` is set, so a part that should only shade, a recipe's `shadow = "only"` on glass, casts the drawn glass's tint rather than an opaque cascade shadow; with `casts_shadow` false it draws nothing, and `render` accepts it. A frame whose glass is all shadow-only runs no glass pass at all (`glass::drawn`). `shadow_only_glass_casts_the_tinted_sun_shadow_of_drawn_glass_and_draws_nothing` (`crates/live/tests/glass_casts.rs`) renders a neutral tinted slab over a floor as shadow-only glass and as a shadow-only `Instance` copy with `set_transmission` and the frames are the same bytes, with the floor beside the slab within 1/255 of the drawn glass's frame.

Glass occludes local lights with the same tint and transmission model as the sun. While a surface casts and a local light is shadowed, the transmission pass draws the casting surfaces once more per light face, after the cascades, into tiles of the transmission map (pass `light transmission`): each face gets a square tile of `transmission_size(face resolution)` texels (256 at the default 1024), packed as many to a layer as the cascade layer's size allows (16 at a 4096 atlas), so two lights' twelve faces take one extra layer. The lights uniform carries the tile size and the tiles per row in `info.zw` (0 while no glass casts into a light), and `light_tint` in `LIGHTS_WGSL` reads the face's tile at the same shifted point `light_shadow` compares: where the shaded point lies behind the nearest glass the light passes the glass's `cast_tint`, multiplied into the light's depth visibility, in the opaque pass and in lit text alike. The light faces' own depth maps never see glass, so a transmissive instance still blocks a light as before. `glass_tints_local_light_shadows_with_its_cast_tint` lights a floor with two shadowed local lights and no sun, and finds each light's share of the floor in the glass's shadow passed by `cast_tint` within 0.002 of the expected ratio (asserted within 0.02; an opaque copy blocks it within 0.003), the same to 1/255 with the glass drawn or shadow-only. On the RX 9060 XT under `pgpu`, at 1920×1080 with a 4096² sun atlas and two lights' twelve 1024² faces, the light tiles cost 0.041 ms every frame, as the sun transmission does while glass casts, against the 0.078 ms the faces' depth takes when the lights move and nothing when they stay cached; the sun transmission with the glass was 0.116 ms (`glass_in_two_local_lights_costs`, lights still and moving).

A glass surface takes the same two world-space clip planes as an instance surface: `glass::Surface::clip` (zero planes cut nothing; a point with `dot(plane.xyz, p) > plane.w` is cut), applied in the glass front pass, in the back pass and in the cast that writes the sun and local-light transmission. Where a back face is cut and the front kept, the back pass puts the glass's exit on the plane along the view ray with the plane's normal, so the part refracts and absorbs like the solid it keeps; where the cut faces the camera nothing is drawn through it, as an open instance cut shows nothing behind its plane. `clipped_glass_draws_and_casts_like_the_part_it_keeps` cuts a slab in half with either plane, sun and a local light on, and compares it with a slab of the kept half's size: the frames are the same bytes with either plane, where the uncut slab differs in 6,502 of 65,536 pixels.

A content layer can cut its instance out: with `InstanceSurface::cutout` set, the prepass, the opaque pass and the sun and local light casters discard where the material's content slot samples an alpha below `Instance::alpha_cutoff` (0.5 by default), outside the content's crop, or everywhere when the material has no content slot, which is the tracer's `PlacementContent::cutout` rule (wave 33) with the instance's own cutoff. The alpha is read bilinearly from the slot's level 0, so the edge follows the image's resolution, not the mesh's, and a card with a transparent image is one quad again, where it used to take a 128×128 grid carrying vertex alpha. The face rule (`InstanceSurface::face`) does not apply to the cut, as in the tracer. Vertex alpha still cuts as before, and a cut caster also tests vertex alpha × opacity, which plain casters never did. A material marked transmissive with `set_transmission` still casts its whole tint into the transmission map: that pass does not read content. The flag rides bit 2 of the instance's `content_face.z` lane, so no binding or buffer layout changes. Batches split on it (`Batch::cut`, `frame::surface_batches`): a cut batch draws through a prepass pipeline compiled with `use_content_cutout`, the opaque pass compiles it in as the `content_cutout` feature, and its casters draw through `shadow_cut_vertex` and `shadow_cut_fragment` in the `live cut shadow layout` (scene, cascade, deformer and lighting groups, so the content slots and map materials keep their group 3 bindings); the cut prepass and caster pipelines are made on the first frame with a cut batch. A part without the flag compiles none of it: its prepass and opaque pipelines are built with `use_content_cutout` off and its casters keep the vertex-only pipeline, so its frames are byte-identical (`tests/opaque_features.rs` compiles the feature into a scene that does not use it and compares). Cut casters key the cascades' static cache by their cutoff, opacity, content placement and slot and by the frame's content epoch, which every `set_content`, `write_content`, `copy_content`, `regenerate_content` and `release_content` advances, so a content write redraws the cached cascades. `set_opaque_off` with `content_cutout` compiles the cut out of the opaque pass only, leaving the prepass and casters cutting, so use it to measure cost, not to look at the frame. `frame::tests::content_alpha_cuts_a_ring_out_of_one_quad_in_every_pass` draws a ring-shaped image on one quad: the hole and the clear corners show the floor and let the sun through, the ring and its shadow show, and every card pixel whose traced alpha is more than 0.05 from the cutoff matches the tracer's rule (4,296 pixels, none differ). On a card scene at 3840×2160 (24 cards of 0.6 m with a 256² ring image over a floor, every card moving so the cascades redraw, `card_scene_costs_with_content_cutouts_and_vertex_alpha_grids`), one quad per card costs 0.364 ms of depth and velocity, 1.485 ms of opaque and 0.019 ms of cascades as plain cards; cut by content, 0.383, 1.548 and 0.032 ms; and as today's 128×128 grids carrying vertex alpha, 0.593, 2.101 and 0.529 ms. Over the three passes the cut costs 0.10 ms more than plain cards and 1.26 ms less than the grids (0.55 ms of it in the opaque pass and 0.50 ms in the cascades). On a desk scene that cuts nothing, nothing changes: the desk built at the base commit and with this change, run one after the other under `pgpu` at 3840×2160 with the camera moving (600 frames, the same baked probes), reads 8.288 and 8.265 ms of opaque and 0.584 and 0.582 ms of depth and velocity, and in both the cascades stay in their static cache, since the desk's casters draw through the same caster pipelines as before.

Ambient light takes the probe grid where a baked grid covers the point, and the sky's L2 SH elsewhere. Indirect specular comes from `reflection_radiance`: SSR, then local probes, then the sky cube, weighted by the environment BRDF fitted to the tracer's GGX (see [The environment BRDF](#the-environment-brdf-wave-69); zero where a material has no specular at all, as the tracer's). The frame binds the two reflection probes nearest the camera's box, and each surface weighs them by its own position in their boxes, with the seam fade; each cube is sampled at its own mip for the roughness, a coat at its own roughness's mip, and the sky's share is scaled by what the probes see of it (see [Coat reflections and sky visibility](#coat-reflections-and-sky-visibility)). The sun is multiplied by the cascades and, under a tree, by the canopy cookie. In `CanopyMode::Procedural` the leaf shade weighs `set_canopy_strength` (0 to 1, default 0.88, today's fixed weight): the shade scales by the strength over 0.88, so 0 is no gobo, 0.5 is half of the full-weight shade in linear light, and the default frame is byte-identical to before (`tests/canopy_strength.rs`). The cookie mode ignores it. The cascades pancake casters nearer the sun than their near plane onto it, so a receiver box fit still sees a window's blinds a metre away; with `Quality::sun_radius_deg` above zero, the sample searches 16 taps for blockers within the widest penumbra the sun's disc can throw (at most 48 texels) and filters 16 taps over the penumbra of the average blocker, else it keeps the 3×3 PCF and frames are unchanged. Against the engine tracer on a desk scene, with the bake's triangles, materials, sky and sun on both sides, live's mean linear luminance under sun and sky is 1.01 of the trace over the frame and 0.97 on the face, term by term; the soft sun filter still shadows sloped lit receivers a little (sun alone on the face, 0.99 with the 3×3 PCF and 0.93 with the disc), which a receiver-plane depth bias that loosens each tap's comparison by its offset times the receiver's slope should remove; `tests/energy.rs` pins a Lambert plane lit through baked probes under a uniform sky, the same plane under the sun alone, and a mirror sphere in a uniform sky to the tracer within 2%. The mirror found the tracer's error: at roughness 0 its GGX lobe (α = 0.0004) is finer than f32 can resolve near the peak and returned 19% more light than a mirror reflects, so the tracer floors roughness and coat roughness at 0.05 (α = 0.0025, a 0.14° blur) and samples the lobe with the same α it evaluates.

**Sun shadows against the trace.** A product's shadow check found three faults in the soft sun on its desk, each now a `shadow::Quality` field. A shadow map keeps only the surface nearest the sun, so under a window's blinds pancaked onto the near plane a lip a few millimetres over a receiver is hidden, the blocker search finds only the blinds, and the penumbra widens to 48 texels and lets in light from between the slats. With a receiver box and the outer cascade, `near_map` (default on) draws cascade 1, otherwise a copy of cascade 0, without pancaking, so the depth clip drops the casters beyond the near plane and it holds the near ones; a soft sample whose search met a pancaked blocker (stored depth 0) and is not already dark also filters cascade 1 at the near blockers' width and keeps the darker result. A receiver outside the box sampled no map and read lit: `outer_scale` (default `shadow::OUTER_SCALE`, 3) fits the last cascade to the box grown by that factor about its centre, every receiver reads cascade 0, and one outside it reads the outer cascade; inside the box frames do not change, since the cascades were identical there, and 0 restores the old fit. The soft sun's taps already follow the receiver plane up to its slope limit, so its normal offset only pushed overhang shadow edges out by about 0.7 mm on that desk: `soft_offset` (default `shadow::SOFT_OFFSET`, 0) scales the normal offset on the soft path where N·L is at least 1/√17 (the slope limit), and more grazing receivers keep the full offset, which `tests/soft_sun.rs`'s tilted planes need. `layered` (default off) tests each filter tap at two depths when the nearest blockers are a minority: at the near layer's width, counting only depths within that layer's band, and at the averaged width, counting only depths nearer the sun than the band. It darkens a thin caster's umbra beside far casters that are inside the map, but on that desk the near map already does that and it costs 0.12 ms there. Each switch at its old value (`layered` false, `outer_scale` 0, `near_map` false, `soft_offset` 1) gives frames byte-identical to before: the desk's sun-only plate built on main (0.18.19) and on this change with the switches off decodes to the same pixels (one SHA-256 for both, and for a second plate drawn in the same run), and `the_new_switches_leave_a_single_layer_inside_the_box_unchanged` compares two penumbra scenes bit for bit with the defaults. The flags ride bits 4 to 6 of the soft taps' flag lane and `soft_offset` its fourth lane, so no binding changes. A caster quad long enough to cross both the near and the far plane is clamped per vertex, which tilts it, and the part past the far plane is clipped; split such geometry.

The offline tracer takes the same local lights: `pfx_trace::lights::LocalLight` has live's fields and units (`LocalLight::point` is the conversion) plus spot and rect shapes, passed as `Detail::lights`. It samples every local light and one power-weighted emissive surface at each bounce, weighs area lights against BSDF sampling with the power heuristic, and traces all shadow rays through one any-hit pass; [trace-lights.md](trace-lights.md) has the details and the checks.

The frame order is: LOD selection and upload, canopy cookie, shadow cascades (deformed, swaying and skinned casters drawn as moving, then prop impostors and creatures into each cascade), sun transmission through tinted thin casters, local light shadows, depth, velocity and ids, opaque shading (colour and view-space normal with roughness), impostors, linear depth, background motion (reprojected from depth wherever no surface wrote an id, so the sky and props carry camera motion), sky, a copy of the HDR image with seven mips, SSR (kept as next frame's history), glass and liquid over that copy, volumes, particles with falling petals, creatures, TAA with glass's reactive mask, then, when the frame has surface text or sharpening, the sharpen pass or a copy of the resolved image into the crisp display image and surface text drawn into that image (deformed glyphs are subdivided 8×8 and pass through `deform_vertex`; `TextSpace::LitSurface` and `LitDeformed` are shaded by the frame's sun, cascades, canopy, probes and local lights in a separate lit pass), depth of field if set (exposure metered after it), the post chain, overlay text, and last the id passes for glass and text that carry an id (see Picks and ids). Text takes plain MSDF runs, rich quads from `pfx_text`'s `layout_spans` (colour, clip, rotation), and icon quads from a padded icon atlas, in surface and overlay modes alike. The sky is drawn before the copy so glass refracts it and SSR samples it. History resets when `Scene.time` goes back or moves on by more than the history gap (`HISTORY_GAP`, 0.25 s, settable with `Renderer::set_history_gap`), so a scripted clock at 30 fps, a fast clip or a dropped window frame keeps it, when the caller's previous view-projection does not continue the last frame (a cut), on resize, on every `set_sky` (a daylight jump) and when a requested sky swaps in. A viewport change keeps it: the resolve reads the history at the rect it was written at.

### Cold start

On an empty Mesa cache each opaque pipeline costs RADV a full compile of the ubershader, 1 to 4 s on the 9950X, and the rest of the renderer's 61 pipelines about 0.4 s together in `Renderer::new`; on a warm cache an opaque pipeline takes 3.5 ms. Timed on a desk scene at 4K, before this change the first frame made six pipelines (three feature sets, each with both cull modes) without SSR, because the SSR history exists only from the second frame, and the second frame made six more with SSR; 12 compiles, 21.5 s to the second frame (median of three, empty cache, other builds running).

Three changes take it to 2.9 s, frames byte for byte the same:

- **SSR is planned.** `Renderer::new` calls `Frame::set_reflections_planned(true)`, so a batch's set carries `ssr` from the first frame. With no history the lighting group binds the black fallback for both SSR textures, `ssr_resolve` finds every guide depth at zero, every weight zero, and returns exactly `vec4f(0.0)`, which is what the compiled-out branch assigns; so frame one draws through the second frame's pipelines and the same bytes (`the_first_frame_draws_the_same_bytes_through_the_reflection_pipelines`, and the desk's three 4K frames hash the same before and after). A camera cut or a resize, which drop the history, no longer compile a set either. A `Frame` used on its own keeps the old rule.
- **One cull mode at a time.** A set no longer makes the two-sided pipeline until a two-sided batch draws with it. The desk has no two-sided batch, so it makes 3 pipelines where it made 12.
- **`Renderer::warm(&[Scene])`** works out the keys each scene's frame would make, through the same packing and feature code the frame runs (`Frame::opaque_keys`), and compiles them on worker threads (`available_parallelism − 1` by default, `set_warm_workers` to change it). The first scene's keys go first, then the later scenes' new ones. It returns in well under a millisecond with a `warm::Warming` the app polls: `progress()` (made and total, for the first scene and for all), `first_ready()`, `done()`, and `wait()` for a headless caller. Finished pipelines reach the frame through a channel it drains on the next frame; a frame that needs one still compiling waits for that one, and one the warm was not asked for compiles on the render thread as before. A worker that dies hands its key back, and the frame compiles it itself. Selected LOD instances are not part of the warm. `Frame::opaque_made_on_render_thread` counts the compiles the warm did not cover. wgpu's `Device` is `Sync`, RADV compiles in the calling thread, and three compiles on three threads take as long as the slowest. The desk's three are ready 2.7 to 3.2 s after the ask, and the first frame then takes 30 ms.

**The pipeline cache.** `Renderer::use_pipeline_cache(path)` opens a `wgpu::PipelineCache` from a file in the app's own data folder, and every opaque pipeline made after it, on the render thread or a worker, goes through it; `save_pipeline_cache()` writes it back (to `path.partial`, then renamed), so an app saves once `Warming::done()`, and again on exit. The file wraps wgpu's data in our own header: a magic, the key (wgpu's `pipeline_cache_key`, which is the backend, vendor and device, then the adapter name, driver, driver info and the engine version), a SHA-256 of the data and its length. A file for another key, cut short, grown, flipped or not ours is reported as `CacheLoad::Ignored` and an empty cache is used, which the next save replaces. wgpu checks its own header too and falls back to an empty cache (`fallback: true`), and the driver checks its own. The device asks for `Features::PIPELINE_CACHE` wherever the adapter offers it (`floor::OPTIONAL_FEATURES`). wgpu supports it on Vulkan only. On Metal, or without the feature, `use_pipeline_cache` returns `CacheLoad::Unsupported` and nothing changes. `create_pipeline_cache` is unsafe because wgpu cannot prove the bytes came from `get_data`; the checksum is our proof.

What it adds over Mesa's cache, measured on the desk with an empty Mesa cache and the saved file (817 KB): the three opaque pipelines take 4 to 5 ms each, and the first frame is ready 45 ms after the ask. With `MESA_SHADER_CACHE_DISABLE=true` it takes 7 ms. So on Linux it survives what empties Mesa's cache: its size limit, shared with every other program (1 GB by default, games evict it), a user or distribution that disables it, and a cleared `~/.cache`. A driver update empties both caches. On Windows, Vulkan drivers keep their own caches as well, but how they keep and evict them is up to each vendor; this file is the one cache the app owns there. Neither cache helps a buyer's first launch. The warm and the fewer variants do. The pipelines `Renderer::new` makes take no cache yet. They cost 0.4 s cold, against 0.14 s warm.

## Picks and ids

`targets.ids` is an `R32Uint` target, one id per pixel, 0 where nothing pickable was drawn. `Renderer::pick(x, y)` copies that pixel and the linear depth with the next frame, and `Renderer::picked()` returns them as a `Picked { x, y, id, depth }` once the frame is done, from a non-blocking poll. Ids are the caller's: `Instance::id` for meshes, `glass::Surface::id` for glass and liquid, and `TextItem::id` and `TextQuadItem::id` for text. Id 0 never writes, so whatever is behind shows through to the pick.

- **Opaque meshes** write their id in the depth and velocity prepass, as before. TAA and the background motion pass read these ids, and only these.
- **Glass** writes its id where its front face is nearest: where the glass pass's own front id is set, the opaque depth is not in front of it and the back face lies behind the front, the same test that decides where the compose pass draws. Glass with id 0 in front of another glass leaves the pick on what the frame wrote before the glass pass, not on the glass behind.
- **Surface text** (`Surface`, `LitSurface`, `Deformed` and `LitDeformed`) writes its id where a glyph's coverage passes the cutout (coverage at least 0.5, `ID_CUTOUT` in the glyph shader, and colour alpha above 0), behind the same depth test as its colour, so text hidden by a mesh in front of it writes nothing. The id is per run or block (`TextItem::id`, `TextQuadItem::id`): set it to the id of the object the text sits on to pick the object, or to the text's own id to pick the text. Each text space draws its ids with the same vertex shader as its colour, so ids and ink cover the same pixels on a deformed sheet.
- **Overlay text** with an id writes it the same way, on top of everything else.

The id writes are separate passes at the end of the frame, after TAA, the post chain and overlay text, each drawing only into `targets.ids` (glass's ids pass reads the glass pass's own targets through the compose layout, so no layout gains a binding). TAA, velocity, the reactive mask and every colour byte are the same as with no ids; a frame with no glass or text id runs none of them. Text ids are drawn after glass ids, as text's colour is drawn over glass's.

For picks without a GPU round trip, `Renderer::text_bounds()` returns a `TextBounds { list, index, id, rect }` for every text item of the last frame: which list it came from (`TextList::Surface`, `SurfaceQuads`, `Overlay`, `OverlayQuads`), its index there, its id, and its screen-space box in pixels, `[left, top, right, bottom]`, clipped to the viewport. `Renderer::text_at(x, y)` returns the topmost box under a pixel (overlay before surface, later items before earlier). The boxes follow each glyph's placement, rotation, clip and turn; deformed text is boxed at its rest pose, without the deformer's offset. `text::screen_bounds(space, glyphs, size)` computes one box for any glyph list. Within a box, `pfx_text::hit`, `marks` and `caret` find the character.

## Render scale and dynamic resolution

A game that renders the 3D frame below the window's size keeps the `Renderer` at the content size and sets its viewport to `ScreenReport::render_size(scale)`, renders into `pfx_live::upscale::Upscale::source_target`, and draws it into the window with `Upscale::encode`: a clamped Catmull-Rom upscale of the frame's top-left rect into the content rect, the aspect policy's bars around it, and a flat HUD at native resolution over it (0.43 ms from 1080p to 4K). `pfx_gpu::screens::DynamicResolution` picks the scale from this renderer's timestamps (`Submitted::arrived`) against a GPU budget, and `Renderer::apply_dynamic_resolution` applies it each frame. Both are in `docs/screens.md`.

**The viewport.** `Renderer::new` and `resize` allocate every screen-sized target at the given size, the capacity (`capacity()`); `set_viewport(width, height)` picks the top-left rect a frame draws into (`size()`), at no cost: no allocation, no pipeline, no history restart. Every pass reads and writes only the rect, with UVs, texel sizes and clamps taken from it; the scene colour mips are built over the rect with a one-texel edge border so mip taps clamp at the rect's edge as they would at a texture's; TAA and SSR reproject their history from the rect it was written at into the current one, so a scale step blends instead of restarting. At the full rect the bytes are unchanged; at a smaller one the frame matches a renderer resized to the rect within 0.016/255. The output need only be as large as the rect. Frames on demand and flat looks draw at the full rect. The details and measurements are in `docs/screens.md` (Viewport dynamic resolution); `Renderer::target_textures()` lists the allocated targets, and `history_restarts()` counts the history's restarts.

## Submission, late timings and the sky's settle

`Renderer::render` encodes a frame, submits it, waits for the GPU and returns that frame's pass timings, exactly as before. `Renderer::submit` takes the same arguments, encodes and submits the frame and returns at once with a `Submitted { frame, arrived }`: the frame's number (counted per renderer from 0) and the timings of earlier frames that completed since the last call. Timestamps are copied into a ring of three readback buffers, each mapped with `map_async` and collected by a non-blocking poll before and after each submit; `Renderer::timings()` is the latest complete set with its frame number. A frame's timings arrive one or two frames late (`TIMING_LAG` is 2). The ring never stalls the caller: a frame that finds every slot in flight has its timings dropped, and a set that comes back more than two frames late is dropped too; `timing_drops()` counts both (`ring`, `late`). `frames_in_flight()` is how many submitted frames the GPU has not finished, and `wait_frames(n)` blocks until at most `n` remain, for a headless caller that wants a swapchain's pacing (`wait_frames(0)` drains; both return the timings that arrived). A windowed app is paced by its surface and needs neither. A submitted frame draws the same bytes as a rendered one: 120 frames through `submit` end on the same image as 120 through `render`, and paced to two frames in flight every frame's timings arrive within two frames. An unpaced loop that outruns the GPU drops timings instead of waiting. Measured on 2026-10-03 on a desk scene at 3840×2160 under `pgpu` with turns, uncapped, without the light bakes (`PFX_UNCAPPED=1`), 600 frames each: `render` holds the calling thread 15.17 ms a frame (p50; p95 20.93 ms) at 61.2 fps; `submit` holds it 0.63 ms (p95 1.07 ms, max 1.49 ms) and, paced to two frames in flight with `wait_frames(2)` and drained before each turn, runs at 64.3 fps (frame p50 13.93 ms against 15.17 ms), with no timings dropped. The scene is GPU-bound, so the gain in frame rate is the CPU's encode now overlapping the GPU; the calling thread is free for the other 13 ms. Auto exposure reads its meter whenever the reading comes back, so with `submit` it adapts a frame or more later than with `render`; a fixed exposure is identical on both paths.

Readbacks stay blocking: `readback_geometry`, the TAA captures and `Gpu::readback_*` submit a copy and wait for it, so they return the last submitted frame. A pick (`pick`, then `picked`) is copied with its frame and comes back once that frame is done, from a non-blocking poll, on either path.

`Renderer::set_sky` (and `Frame::set_sky(.., true)`) still builds the sky's SH and GGX cube on the calling thread before it returns, with the same result as before. `Renderer::request_sky(source, hour)` (and `Frame::set_sky(.., false)`) never blocks: it records the request and returns its number. At the start of the next frame the newest request is handed to the sky's worker thread, so many requests within one frame build only the last; while the worker builds, a newer request replaces any waiting one, so a scrubber calling it every frame keeps only its newest hour and never starves. The worker computes the SH in the stepped order and the six cube faces on six scoped threads, converts and uploads the HDR and every cube mip into new textures, and the frame that finds it done swaps the background, SH and cube in whole, sets the probe hour that came with the request and resets history. Until then the old sky lights every frame, so no frame shows a half-built cube. The stepped result is bit for bit the settled one: the same per-texel prefilter and the same SH summation order. `sky_settling()` says whether a request is still on its way and `wait_sky()` blocks until it is shown. A `set_sky` with settle cancels any request in flight.

Measured in a release build: a `request_sky` call takes about 0.6 µs on the calling thread (mean over 21 calls, 5 µs at most), and the 128-edge analytic sky swaps in about 31 ms later, where `set_sky` with settle blocks for 109 ms.

## Frames on demand

A product whose camera barely moves should not redraw a 4K frame every vsync. With `Renderer::set_frames_on_demand(Some(Limits::default()))` the renderer keeps the last full frame and answers, each tick, what the next one needs; `set_frames_on_demand(None)` and the plain `render`/`submit` calls keep today's behaviour (a product's `Redraw::Always`). It needs an sRGB output format (the display path), and refuses `Rgba16Float` outputs. It draws at the renderer's full size: it refuses a viewport smaller than the capacity, and `set_viewport` refuses one while it is on.

With `PFX_FRAME_STATS=<path>` set, every frame the renderer submits, on demand or not, writes one JSON line with its kind (full, partial, reproject, bridge or idle) and its sim, submit and GPU time ([frame-stats.md](frame-stats.md)).

```rust
let mut next = Next::new(&scene, &text, &effects, finish);
next.hud = hud;            // a FlatScene drawn over the frame, if any
next.content = revision;   // bump when content the renderer can't see changed (content textures)
next.overlay = hud_revision; // bump when the HUD changed
next.moving = &animated;   // screen rects of HUD animations
match renderer.frame_needed(&next) {
    Need::None => {}       // present nothing; the last image stands
    need => { renderer.submit_needed(&next, &need, &surface)?; }
}
```

`frame_needed(&next) -> Need` returns one of four answers:

- **`None`**: nothing differs from the frame on screen. No submission at all: the GPU idles. `present_last(output)` re-presents the last image for a product that must present every vsync (a blit, 0.18 ms at 4K).
- **`Reproject`**: only the camera moved, and by less than the limits. The cached frame is warped by its depth to the new camera.
- **`Partial(regions)`**: the camera is where the cached frame was and only time-driven layers (steam, particles) or the overlay changed; only those rects are redrawn over the cached frame.
- **`Full`**: anything else.

### What counts as a change

The renderer fingerprints, every tick, everything a full frame draws from, and compares it with the cached frame's print:

- the scene: every instance (mesh, model, material, id, opacity, cutoff, deformer, age, coverage, flags), every material, every deformer, the sun, the seed and the wind's strength and direction (not its clock);
- surface text: atlas, colour, id, glyphs and each space without its camera part (a `Surface` space's model is recovered as `VP⁻¹·MVP` and compared within 1e-4, so camera drift never reads as moved text);
- glass surfaces and impostor props;
- the finish and every look setter: each `set_*` on the renderer that changes the image (finish, tape, lens, haze, exposure, sky, probes, ambient, contact, reflection occlusion, sky visibility, probe emission, lights, light quality, transmission, maps, detail, props, tree, canopy, volume cuts, shadow quality, sharpening, late surface text, texture filtering, meshes, glass meshes, the LOD set, resize) bumps a revision; lens, haze, exposure, tape, sharpening, late text and texture filtering only when the value differs, so a product may set them every tick;
- `Next::content`, the product's own revision for what the renderer can't see.

A change makes the next frame `Full` and then keeps `Limits::settle` (8, one TAA jitter cycle) more full frames so the history converges before the frame goes still. Time-driven work that only a full frame draws is *ticking* and keeps every frame full: creatures, liquid, a tree (sway, petals, the canopy gobo), any wind with strength above zero, a running tape glitch, a sky still settling, and auto exposure while it moves (more than 0.1% between full frames). Room haze is held still between full frames: its drift is about 2 cm a second and its per-frame jitter is TAA's to settle, so an idle frame keeps the last one. Time alone changes nothing.

Steam volumes and particles are *layers*: in on-demand mode a full frame draws them after the TAA resolve, the sharpening and the late surface text, over a copy of that image (the *still*), with the unjittered camera, then runs depth of field and the post on the result. Their noise is continuous in time and seeded per scene, not per frame, so they need no TAA; a partial frame redraws exactly what a full one draws. The first frame with a new layer is full, so the still exists. Overlay text and the HUD go into their own layer, drawn only when the overlay's print (its text, spaces and `Next::overlay`) changes, and are composited over the frame in the same pass that presents it.

### Reprojection

The warp is one full-screen pass from the cached colour and its linear depth to the new camera. For each output pixel it solves `F(s) = goal` by fixed-point iteration, where `F` sends a cached pixel through its depth to the new view (one affine ray, one fused matrix: about 12 FMAs a step), stopping under 0.05 px. A min/max depth map of 4×4 tiles, each widened by its eight neighbours, built once per full frame (`demand tiles`), says whether the pixel is near a depth edge; there it also solves from the nearest and farthest depths and keeps the nearest hit, and where nothing converges it takes the background solution, which fills the disocclusion from its neighbours. Colour is resampled with a 5-tap Catmull-Rom clamped to the range of its own five taps, and a pixel that lands within 0.002 px of a texel centre loads that texel exactly, so a warp onto the cached camera reproduces the cached frame byte for byte. Sky pixels move as directions.

The clamp to the taps' own range replaced a clamp to the 2×2 neighbourhood around the sample (wave 83), so the colour takes five filtered fetches where it took nine. The warp is bound by its filtered fetches, not by bytes: with one bilinear fetch in place of the Catmull-Rom it ran in 0.291 ms. Measured at 3840×2160 under `pgpu run --class clip`, the builds paired in one job at the same clock (1.5–1.8 GHz), three runs each, per reprojected frame without and with the HUD line: 0.487 / 0.510 ms before, 0.470 / 0.500 ms after. In `reprojection_error_stays_small_inside_the_limits` the mean error moves by at most 0.001/255 and mostly falls, and the visible share rises by at most 0.011 points (pitch 0.25°: 0.202 to 0.197, 0.302% to 0.296%; zoom 1%: 0.278% to 0.289%). Two variants were rejected:

- An 8-bit copy of the cached colour (`Rgba8UnormSrgb`, written by the full frame's present as a second target). With nine fetches it saved nothing (0.488 / 0.510 ms); with five it gave 0.463 / 0.491 ms, only 0.007–0.009 ms better than the float colour, and it added 0.07 ms to every full frame's present (0.215 to 0.285 ms). It also needed the float colour beside it where an overlay pixel is partly transparent, to keep the warp onto the cached camera exact.
- A packed `R32Uint` copy at gamma 2, read through four gathers (the full 4×4 kernel, four fetches). Unpacking and weighting sixteen texels made the pass ALU-bound at 0.796 ms, and quantizing in another curve than the output's doubled the mean error (orbit 0.3°: 0.141 to 0.267).

A reprojected frame is replaced by a full one when any limit is passed:

### Partial redraw

A partial frame copies the still into each region, draws the layers (the volume scissored to its box's screen bounds plus 8 px, which its region covers with a 4 px margin), runs depth of field and the post over the layer into a scratch image, and copies only the regions into the cached colour, which is then presented with the overlay. Regions closer than 16 px merge, and above 60% of the screen they become the whole screen. Each layer region also grows by the finish's reach, `GpuChain::region_reach()` (below), since a finish that reads beyond the pixel changes the frame that far from the layer: a Kuwahara of radius 3 grows it by 3 px. The post over a grown rect reads up to the reach beyond it again, and those pixels of the layer image are the still, because every earlier layer draw stayed inside an earlier region and a partial frame copies the still over this frame's regions and the last frame's. A finish whose reach is unknown (bloom with strength, a halation, outlines, distortion, the tape) or a lens makes every layer region the whole screen: still no scene draw, only the layer and the post. A pointwise chain the post cannot run by region (a bloom at strength 0) keeps its rects and posts in full. A partial frame posts with the cached frame's number, so grain holds still with the rest of the frame.

The post runs over the rects only, through `GpuChain::run_region(…, rects, …)`: each stage is a compute pass over the rect (a workgroup of 8×8 covers it and the shader's scissor, `post.region`, drops the pixels outside), grown by the reach of the stages after it, so a stage reads only what the one before it wrote. Whether a chain can do this is decided once per chain by `GpuChain::region_reach()`, which is the sum of the reaches of its passes, or `None`. Every group must be one stage from the image to the image and every pass must have a known reach: the per-pixel passes and the fused grade (0), Cel, Rim, Cavity and the fused cavity and rim (0: their normals come in with the frame, not from an earlier stage), the one-bit, halftone, dust and scratches passes (0), the watercolor (1, its Sobel), Kuwahara (its radius, at most 8), weave (twice its amount, rounded up, plus 1) and aberration (its amount, rounded up, plus 1). A chain with a bloom or a halation (the pyramid reads the whole frame), a neon glow, the tape, distortion or curvature (which sample anywhere), pixelate (blocks), or any pass that reads depth (outline, comic, rubber hose, modern comic, tilt shift) returns `None`, and `run_region` then runs the whole post and returns `false`; the caller's copy out of the scratch image is unchanged either way. A region run writes nothing outside its rects, and inside them it is the full run's pixels (`region_post_equals_the_full_post_inside_its_rects`, on a neutral synthetic image with one and several rects, at the image's edge, overlapping and past it, over five chains; the largest difference is 0). ### Waking the GPU

GPU clocks follow load: on the RX 9060 XT (500, current, 2780 MHz levels in `pp_dpm_sclk`) a light frame rate leaves the shader clock low, so the same frame costs more milliseconds of GPU at idle-like loads than at full load. Two mitigations are the product's choice:

- `Renderer::wake(groups)` submits a cheap ALU compute pass (`demand::WAKE_GROUPS` workgroups) the moment input arrives, before the product builds the frame.
- `Renderer::submit_bridge(&next, output)` shows the cached frame warped to the new camera at once (no limits applied), while the full frame renders into the next image.

Measured at 3840×2160 (`frames --large --wake`): full frames at 60 fps with the camera jumping 2° every other frame, then an idle of the given length with no submission, then the input: the variant, then a full frame. Medians of five; the clock is read from `pp_dpm_sclk` right after the idle and right after the frame.

| Idle | sclk after the idle | Full GPU ms, plain | with `wake` | with `wake` ×8 | Bridge on screen | Full frame done |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1.6–1.8 GHz | 4.89 | 4.93 | 4.94 | 0.9 ms (0.60 GPU) | 7.3–7.8 ms |
| 0.1 s | 1.6–1.8 GHz | 4.92 | 4.92 | 4.89 | 1.0 ms | 7.7–8.1 ms |
| 0.3 s | 1.4–1.7 GHz | 4.95 | 4.95 | 4.90 | 1.1 ms | 7.7–8.3 ms |
| 1 s | 1.4–1.6 GHz | 4.93 | 4.93 | 4.89 | 1.1 ms | 8.5–9.8 ms |
| 2.5 s | 1.4–1.6 GHz | 4.98 | 4.97 | 4.89 | 1.1 ms | 8.7–9.5 ms |

Steady full frames between the trials take 4.93 ms (p50), so on this desk scene and this machine the first frame after up to 2.5 s of idle costs no more GPU time than a steady one, and the clock read after the idle never fell below 1.4 GHz; `wake` (0.18 ms of GPU for 1,024 groups) changes nothing measurable. The clock does move the cost: with other work holding it near 3.4 GHz the same full frame took 3.97 ms, against 4.9 ms near 1.7 GHz, a ratio like the 5.0 against 8.4 ms a product reported from one opaque pass. Idles of 3 s and more could not be measured under pgpu, which lends an unused GPU to the next job after 3 s and returns it only after that job's turn. The bridge is the mitigation that shows: a frame for the new camera is on screen about 1 ms after the input, 6–8 ms before the full one.

## Risks and decisions still open

| Risk or question | Prototype/decision needed |
|---|---|
| Native 4K shading and bandwidth exceed the estimate | Timestamp each pass on the target GPU/driver with real shipping scenes before fixing quality tiers; investigate overdraw, formats and upload stalls. |
| TAA ghosts curled text, thin foliage, liquid and chrome | Build motion-vector, disocclusion and reactive-mask reference clips; compare to native 4× MSAA. |
| Analytic refraction and caustics lose their look | Measure the scissored SDF bridge, then test explicit front/back meshes and caustic textures against offline frames. |
| Baked irradiance leaks, or daylight anchors pop | Compare a six-lobe grid with visibility-aware bricks and directional lightmaps; decide anchor times from measured image error. |
| Material parity is only nominal | Define numeric test swatches for IOR, roughness, transmission, coat and ageing in the shared material step; review linear HDR outputs before grade. |
| Asset size and adapter contracts are unsettled | Confirm KTX2/BC feature support, package budgets, scene snapshot versioning and bake manifest fields with the apps that use them. |
| Effect counts and 2-million-triangle cap may be wrong | Profile representative scenes; tune scene caps without reducing native output or hiding visible curvature. |

This document does not prove 60 fps or settle precise scene caps, final bake anchor count, probe layout, compressed-format support, scroll tessellation limits, or the exact visual tolerance for each product. Those are measured prototype decisions. The first implementation milestone is a 4K scene with GPU pass timestamps and matched offline reference frames.
